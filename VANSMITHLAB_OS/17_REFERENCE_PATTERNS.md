# Reference Patterns — Research on Similar Resources

## Purpose

These resources are studied for structural patterns, not copied visually or editorially. VANSMITHLAB must remain a distinct, independent encyclopedia of design and visual culture.

## 1. The Met — Heilbrunn Timeline of Art History

The Met combines thematic essays with chronological and geographical exploration; it describes the resource as a reference, research and teaching tool and publishes hundreds of chronologies alongside more than a thousand essays.

**Adopt:** multiple discovery dimensions (theme/time/place), expert-style essays, chronology as a first-class interface, visible institutional context.
**Avoid:** making the site collection-dependent; VANSMITHLAB must connect design knowledge beyond a single museum collection.

Source: [Heilbrunn Timeline of Art History](https://www.metmuseum.org/es/essays/timeline-of-art-history).

## 2. Fashion History Timeline — Fashion Institute of Technology

FIT pairs decade/century overviews with designer profiles, thematic essays, garment analysis, film analysis, a visual dictionary and a research-source database. It positions itself as an accessible starting point for fashion-history research while acknowledging that the work evolves.

**Adopt:** explicit page formats; a glossary as a serious learning tool; overview pages that link evidence, objects and vocabulary; visible work-in-progress/correction posture.
**Avoid:** limiting the model to fashion history. VANSMITHLAB extends into the full designed environment and contemporary visual culture.

Source: [About the Fashion History Timeline](https://fashionhistory.fitnyc.edu/about-timeline/), [Fashion History Timeline](https://fashionhistory.fitnyc.edu/).

## 3. Google Arts & Culture — Fashion projects

Google Arts & Culture connects many partner institutions through thematic paths such as making, materials, fashion movements, art, dress, technology and conservation. The fashion area demonstrates that readers can move between object records, stories, exhibitions, images and video without first understanding a museum database.

**Adopt:** theme-led exploratory routes, cross-disciplinary links, image/video as a doorway to knowledge and plural cultural perspectives.
**Avoid:** infinite browsing without an evidence/trust surface. VANSMITHLAB pages must expose provenance, claims and sources.

Source: [The Making Of](https://artsandculture.google.com/project/fashion-making-of), [Fashion + The Arts](https://artsandculture.google.com/project/fashion-art), [Fashion movements](https://artsandculture.google.com/project/fashion-movements/en).

## 4. Vitra Design Museum

Vitra frames design through its relationship to architecture, art, technology and everyday culture, rather than isolating furniture as a product category.

**Adopt:** an expansive but coherent definition of design and interdisciplinary treatment.
**Avoid:** a museum-like object vault without explanatory routes for a general audience.

Source: [Vitra Design Museum](https://www.vitra.com/en-us/campus/architecture/architecture-vitra-design-museum).

## Resulting product position

VANSMITHLAB combines: the temporal rigor of The Met, the structured fashion-learning formats of FIT, the thematic bridges of Google Arts & Culture, and the interdisciplinary definition of design associated with Vitra. Its differentiator is the AI-native, claim-and-citation knowledge engine with Russian as the editorial master, explicit uncertainty, versioned corrections and autonomous multilingual publication.
