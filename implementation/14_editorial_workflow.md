\# Editorial Workflow

Version: 1.0



\## Purpose



This document defines the complete editorial workflow of VAN SMITH LAB.



It describes how knowledge moves from an idea to a published knowledge object while maintaining editorial quality, verification standards and structural consistency.



The workflow is the operational backbone of the project.



\---



\## Objectives



The editorial workflow shall:



\- Standardize content production

\- Maintain factual accuracy

\- Preserve editorial consistency

\- Support AI-assisted production

\- Ensure traceability

\- Minimize manual repetition



\---



\## Principles



The workflow shall be:



\- Repeatable

\- Transparent

\- Versioned

\- Auditable

\- AI-assisted

\- Human-supervised



Publication always requires successful validation.

\# Stage 1 — Topic Selection



Input:



\- Editorial idea

\- User request

\- Research topic

\- Industry event

\- Official announcement



Output:



Editorial Task



\---



\# Stage 2 — Research



Responsibilities:



\- Collect information

\- Identify entities

\- Identify terminology

\- Discover related knowledge

\- Build research dataset



Deliverable:



Research Package



\---



\# Stage 3 — Source Collection



Collect:



\- Official websites

\- Academic publications

\- Technical documentation

\- Interviews

\- Books

\- Reliable journalism



Every source receives metadata.



\---



\# Stage 4 — Verification



Verification includes:



\- Fact comparison

\- Date verification

\- Name verification

\- Relationship verification

\- Source consistency



Output:



Verified Dataset



\---



\# Stage 5 — Knowledge Modeling



Convert verified information into:



\- Knowledge Object

\- Categories

\- Tags

\- Relationships

\- Metadata



Editorial structure follows the Knowledge Model.



\---



\# Stage 6 — Writing



Create:



\- Front Matter

\- Editorial content

\- Internal links

\- References

\- Summary



Writing remains factual and neutral.



\---



\# Stage 7 — Editorial Review



Editors verify:



\- Accuracy

\- Structure

\- Language

\- Consistency

\- Completeness



Editorial review precedes publication.



\---



\# Stage 8 — AI Processing



AI may assist with:



\- Metadata

\- Translation

\- Summaries

\- Relationship suggestions

\- SEO

\- Media generation



AI output always remains reviewable.



\---



\# Stage 9 — Validation



Validation checks:



\- Front Matter

\- Schema

\- Sources

\- Relationships

\- Internal Links

\- Metadata

\- Required Sections



Validation failures stop publication.



\---



\# Stage 10 — Publication



Publishing performs:



\- Content generation

\- Search indexing

\- Graph update

\- Sitemap update

\- Cache refresh



Publication creates a new version.



\---



\# Stage 11 — Monitoring



After publication:



\- Monitor official sources

\- Monitor corrections

\- Monitor updates

\- Detect outdated information



Monitoring creates update tasks.



\---



\# Stage 12 — Revision



Revision may include:



\- New facts

\- Corrections

\- Expanded content

\- Improved references

\- Updated relationships



Each revision creates a new version.



\---

Supported statuses:

Verification

Status changes are recorded automatically.



\---



\# Editorial Roles



Roles include:



Researcher



Editor



Reviewer



AI Assistant



Publisher



Validator



One person may perform multiple roles.



\---



\# AI Responsibilities



AI may:



\- Draft content

\- Suggest metadata

\- Detect relationships

\- Generate translations

\- Produce summaries



AI never becomes the editorial authority.



\---



\# Human Responsibilities



Humans remain responsible for:



\- Verification

\- Editorial approval

\- Publication

\- Corrections

\- Final quality



Editorial responsibility cannot be delegated.



\---



\# Traceability



Every editorial action records:



\- Timestamp

\- User

\- AI Agent

\- Version

\- Change Summary



Editorial history remains permanent.



\---



\# Quality Assurance



Quality checks include:



\- Editorial consistency

\- Knowledge consistency

\- Source quality

\- Language quality

\- Structural validation



Every publication passes quality assurance.



\---



\# Rollback



Published content may be reverted.



Rollback restores:



\- Content

\- Metadata

\- Relationships

\- Search Index



History is preserved.



\---



\# Automation



Automation may perform:



\- Metadata generation

\- Search indexing

\- Graph generation

\- Validation

\- Build

\- Deployment



Automation never bypasses validation.



\---



\# Performance



The workflow should minimize repetitive work while preserving editorial quality.



Automation supports editors rather than replacing them.



\---



\# Extensibility



Additional editorial stages may be introduced without affecting completed publications.



Existing workflow history remains unchanged.



\---



\# Consistency



The Editorial Workflow follows:



\- Manifest

\- Editorial Policy

\- Source Policy

\- Verification Policy

\- Knowledge Model

\- AI Pipeline

\- Architecture



The Editorial Workflow defines the canonical process by which every piece of knowledge is created, verified, published and maintained within VAN SMITH LAB.
