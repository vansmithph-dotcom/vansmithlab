## 1. Origins and context

COLLINS was founded in New York in 2008. Its founders include Brian Collins and Leland Maschmeyer. The organisation emerged within a professional context in which tools, markets and cultural expectations were changing the definition of the discipline itself. COLLINS entered that process not as an isolated author but as a collective structure capable of turning a method into repeatable practice.[^clm_origin]

## 2. How the method works

The core principle can be stated simply: connects cultural research, typography, narrative and system building so visual language supports a new organisational position. This matters because method defines not an exterior style but a way of making decisions: what to research, which constraints matter, where conflict lies and how form connects to use. The repeatability of that approach is what makes institutional influence visible.[^clm_method]

## 3. San Francisco Symphony: first anchor example

San Francisco Symphony. The symphony identity used responsive typography and digital media to present classical music as living contemporary culture. The example matters as observable evidence of method: the relationship between problem and form can be inspected in a specific object, image, space or system rather than accepted from studio rhetoric.[^clm_case1]

## 4. Figma: second anchor example

Figma. Work with technology companies shows an approach in which identity needs to be as flexible as the product and community around it. The second example tests whether the approach remains coherent when scale, client, medium and cultural context change. It is therefore evidence of a working system rather than an addition to a list of famous projects.[^clm_case2]

## 5. A system rather than a single device

With COLLINS, it is more useful to look for a system of choices than one repeated visual gesture. Connects cultural research, typography, narrative and system building so visual language supports a new organisational position. Similarity between projects may therefore lie in research procedure, team organisation, material attitude or sequence of work rather than identical form. VANSMITHLAB records authorship through mechanism rather than superficial resemblance.

## 6. Influence on the discipline

Became a prominent example of a new American practice where strategy and expressive graphic design are not treated as opposites. Influence becomes visible when questions and working methods enter the practice of other authors, schools, clients or institutions. This does not necessarily mean direct imitation; more often the range of acceptable solutions and the professional vocabulary itself changes.[^clm_impact]

## 7. Critical distance

A strong visual identity is not the same as a strong organisation. The profile separates design quality from broader claims of business transformation that require different evidence. This matters for COLLINS because reputation can turn successful decisions into universal mythology. VANSMITHLAB separates documented fact, interpretation and editorial judgment: influence is recognised without replacing analysis of limits, context and collective authorship.

## 8. Why the organisation matters now

COLLINS works from New York and San Francisco across cultural, technology and consumer organisations.[^clm_current] The relevance of the profile is not defined only by current commissions. The practice remains a useful comparison point because it reveals which professional questions survive changing technologies and fashions and which belonged to a specific historical moment.
