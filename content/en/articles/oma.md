## 1. Origins and context

OMA was founded in Rotterdam in 1975. Its founders include Rem Koolhaas, Elia Zenghelis, Madelon Vriesendorp and Zoe Zenghelis. The organisation emerged within a professional context in which tools, markets and cultural expectations were changing the definition of the discipline itself. OMA entered that process not as an isolated author but as a collective structure capable of turning a method into repeatable practice.[^clm_origin]

## 2. How the method works

The core principle can be stated simply: treats architecture as work with programme, density, infrastructure and cultural contradiction; its research arm AMO extends the method beyond buildings. This matters because method defines not an exterior style but a way of making decisions: what to research, which constraints matter, where conflict lies and how form connects to use. The repeatability of that approach is what makes institutional influence visible.[^clm_method]

## 3. Seattle Central Library: first anchor example

Seattle Central Library. The library is organised as a sequence of programme platforms and public routes, turning the type into an open urban infrastructure. The example matters as observable evidence of method: the relationship between problem and form can be inspected in a specific object, image, space or system rather than accepted from studio rhetoric.[^clm_case1]

## 4. CCTV Headquarters: second anchor example

CCTV Headquarters. The Beijing loop rejected the conventional tower in favour of a continuous volume physically connecting different parts of the organisation. The second example tests whether the approach remains coherent when scale, client, medium and cultural context change. It is therefore evidence of a working system rather than an addition to a list of famous projects.[^clm_case2]

## 5. A system rather than a single device

With OMA, it is more useful to look for a system of choices than one repeated visual gesture. Treats architecture as work with programme, density, infrastructure and cultural contradiction; its research arm AMO extends the method beyond buildings. Similarity between projects may therefore lie in research procedure, team organisation, material attitude or sequence of work rather than identical form. VANSMITHLAB records authorship through mechanism rather than superficial resemblance.

## 6. Influence on the discipline

Made programme analysis, diagrams and urban research autonomous tools of architectural design. Influence becomes visible when questions and working methods enter the practice of other authors, schools, clients or institutions. This does not necessarily mean direct imitation; more often the range of acceptable solutions and the professional vocabulary itself changes.[^clm_impact]

## 7. Critical distance

Architectural fame often concentrates on the photographable image of a building. The stronger test is how concept works with programme, structure, material, city and actual use. This matters for OMA because reputation can turn successful decisions into universal mythology. VANSMITHLAB separates documented fact, interpretation and editorial judgment: influence is recognised without replacing analysis of limits, context and collective authorship.

## 8. Why the organisation matters now

OMA officially operates through an international partnership and several global offices, while AMO continues research and advisory work.[^clm_current] The relevance of the profile is not defined only by current commissions. The practice remains a useful comparison point because it reveals which professional questions survive changing technologies and fashions and which belonged to a specific historical moment.
