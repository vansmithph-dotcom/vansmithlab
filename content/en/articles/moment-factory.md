## 1. Origins and context

Moment Factory was founded in Montreal in 2001. The organisation emerged within a professional context in which tools, markets and cultural expectations were changing the definition of the discipline itself. Moment Factory entered that process not as an isolated author but as a collective structure capable of turning a method into repeatable practice.[^clm_origin]

## 2. How the method works

The core principle can be stated simply: combines light, sound, video, scenography, architecture and interaction in large public and entertainment environments. This matters because method defines not an exterior style but a way of making decisions: what to research, which constraints matter, where conflict lies and how form connects to use. The repeatability of that approach is what makes institutional influence visible.[^clm_method]

## 3. Lumina: first anchor example

Lumina. Night walks use landscape, light and sound as one dramaturgical environment in which the digital layer amplifies rather than replaces the place. The example matters as observable evidence of method: the relationship between problem and form can be inspected in a specific object, image, space or system rather than accepted from studio rhetoric.[^clm_case1]

## 4. Concert staging: second anchor example

Concert staging. Live-music work demonstrates the need to synchronise technology with performance rhythm and large-audience behaviour. The second example tests whether the approach remains coherent when scale, client, medium and cultural context change. It is therefore evidence of a working system rather than an addition to a list of famous projects.[^clm_case2]

## 5. A system rather than a single device

With Moment Factory, it is more useful to look for a system of choices than one repeated visual gesture. Combines light, sound, video, scenography, architecture and interaction in large public and entertainment environments. Similarity between projects may therefore lie in research procedure, team organisation, material attitude or sequence of work rather than identical form. VANSMITHLAB records authorship through mechanism rather than superficial resemblance.

## 6. Influence on the discipline

Became a major example of multimedia installation developing into a durable international production discipline. Influence becomes visible when questions and working methods enter the practice of other authors, schools, clients or institutions. This does not necessarily mean direct imitation; more often the range of acceptable solutions and the professional vocabulary itself changes.[^clm_impact]

## 7. Critical distance

Technical complexity does not automatically create a meaningful experience. The key question is whether technology clarifies the idea and space or merely demonstrates equipment. This matters for Moment Factory because reputation can turn successful decisions into universal mythology. VANSMITHLAB separates documented fact, interpretation and editorial judgment: influence is recognised without replacing analysis of limits, context and collective authorship.

## 8. Why the organisation matters now

Moment Factory continues to create permanent and temporary projects worldwide.[^clm_current] The relevance of the profile is not defined only by current commissions. The practice remains a useful comparison point because it reveals which professional questions survive changing technologies and fashions and which belonged to a specific historical moment.
