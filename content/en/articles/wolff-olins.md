## 1. Origins and context

Wolff Olins was founded in London in 1965. Its founders include Michael Wolff and Wally Olins. The organisation emerged within a professional context in which tools, markets and cultural expectations were changing the definition of the discipline itself. Wolff Olins entered that process not as an isolated author but as a collective structure capable of turning a method into repeatable practice.[^clm_origin]

## 2. How the method works

The core principle can be stated simply: builds identity around an organisation’s strategic idea and accepts systems that may be variable, contentious and deliberately open-ended. This matters because method defines not an exterior style but a way of making decisions: what to research, which constraints matter, where conflict lies and how form connects to use. The repeatability of that approach is what makes institutional influence visible.[^clm_method]

## 3. Tate: first anchor example

Tate. The identity used many blurred versions of the name, turning the museum from one fixed mark into a changing field of contemporary art. The example matters as observable evidence of method: the relationship between problem and form can be inspected in a specific object, image, space or system rather than accepted from studio rhetoric.[^clm_case1]

## 4. London 2012: second anchor example

London 2012. The angular Olympic emblem generated intense public debate and became an example of deliberately unconventional identity for a global event. The second example tests whether the approach remains coherent when scale, client, medium and cultural context change. It is therefore evidence of a working system rather than an addition to a list of famous projects.[^clm_case2]

## 6. Influence on the discipline

Expanded branding into a way of changing organisational behaviour and self-definition rather than appearance alone. Influence becomes visible when questions and working methods enter the practice of other authors, schools, clients or institutions. This does not necessarily mean direct imitation; more often the range of acceptable solutions and the professional vocabulary itself changes.[^clm_impact]

## 7. Critical distance

A strong visual identity is not the same as a strong organisation. The profile separates design quality from broader claims of business transformation that require different evidence. This matters for Wolff Olins because reputation can turn successful decisions into universal mythology. VANSMITHLAB separates documented fact, interpretation and editorial judgment: influence is recognised without replacing analysis of limits, context and collective authorship.

## 8. Why the organisation matters now

Wolff Olins continues to operate as an international strategy and identity studio.[^clm_current] The relevance of the profile is not defined only by current commissions. The practice remains a useful comparison point because it reveals which professional questions survive changing technologies and fashions and which belonged to a specific historical moment.
