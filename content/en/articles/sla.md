## 1. Origins and context

SLA was founded in Copenhagen in 1994. Its founders include Stig L. Andersson. The organisation emerged within a professional context in which tools, markets and cultural expectations were changing the definition of the discipline itself. SLA entered that process not as an isolated author but as a collective structure capable of turning a method into repeatable practice.[^clm_origin]

## 2. How the method works

The core principle can be stated simply: uses nature as active urban infrastructure for climate, biodiversity, water, shade and everyday human life. This matters because method defines not an exterior style but a way of making decisions: what to research, which constraints matter, where conflict lies and how form connects to use. The repeatability of that approach is what makes institutional influence visible.[^clm_method]

## 3. Climate-adaptive urban space: first anchor example

Climate-adaptive urban space. The studio’s projects show how stormwater, planting and public use can be designed as one system rather than separate engineering layers. The example matters as observable evidence of method: the relationship between problem and form can be inspected in a specific object, image, space or system rather than accepted from studio rhetoric.[^clm_case1]

## 4. Nature-based design: second anchor example

Nature-based design. Rather than decorative planting, SLA builds long-term ecosystems in which species selection and growth conditions form part of the architectural concept. The second example tests whether the approach remains coherent when scale, client, medium and cultural context change. It is therefore evidence of a working system rather than an addition to a list of famous projects.[^clm_case2]

## 5. A system rather than a single device

With SLA, it is more useful to look for a system of choices than one repeated visual gesture. Uses nature as active urban infrastructure for climate, biodiversity, water, shade and everyday human life. Similarity between projects may therefore lie in research procedure, team organisation, material attitude or sequence of work rather than identical form. VANSMITHLAB records authorship through mechanism rather than superficial resemblance.

## 6. Influence on the discipline

Helped make biodiversity and climate adaptation central themes of contemporary urban landscape practice. Influence becomes visible when questions and working methods enter the practice of other authors, schools, clients or institutions. This does not necessarily mean direct imitation; more often the range of acceptable solutions and the professional vocabulary itself changes.[^clm_impact]

## 7. Critical distance

Landscape unfolds over time, so opening-day appearance says little about long-term quality. Growth, maintenance, climate resilience, access and actual public use matter. This matters for SLA because reputation can turn successful decisions into universal mythology. VANSMITHLAB separates documented fact, interpretation and editorial judgment: influence is recognised without replacing analysis of limits, context and collective authorship.

## 8. Why the organisation matters now

SLA operates internationally with a focus on nature-based urban design.[^clm_current] The relevance of the profile is not defined only by current commissions. The practice remains a useful comparison point because it reveals which professional questions survive changing technologies and fashions and which belonged to a specific historical moment.
