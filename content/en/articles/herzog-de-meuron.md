## 1. Origins and context

Herzog & de Meuron was founded in Basel in 1978. Its founders include Jacques Herzog and Pierre de Meuron. The organisation emerged within a professional context in which tools, markets and cultural expectations were changing the definition of the discipline itself. Herzog & de Meuron entered that process not as an isolated author but as a collective structure capable of turning a method into repeatable practice.[^clm_origin]

## 2. How the method works

The core principle can be stated simply: uses material, surface and construction as carriers of architectural image while rethinking building types through specific contexts and existing fabric. This matters because method defines not an exterior style but a way of making decisions: what to research, which constraints matter, where conflict lies and how form connects to use. The repeatability of that approach is what makes institutional influence visible.[^clm_method]

## 3. Tate Modern: first anchor example

Tate Modern. The transformation of Bankside Power Station retained its industrial mass and showed how a cultural institution can emerge through precise adaptation. The example matters as observable evidence of method: the relationship between problem and form can be inspected in a specific object, image, space or system rather than accepted from studio rhetoric.[^clm_case1]

## 4. Elbphilharmonie: second anchor example

Elbphilharmonie. The Hamburg concert hall placed a new glass public architecture above a heavy warehouse, making the junction of old and new its defining image. The second example tests whether the approach remains coherent when scale, client, medium and cultural context change. It is therefore evidence of a working system rather than an addition to a list of famous projects.[^clm_case2]

## 5. A system rather than a single device

With Herzog & de Meuron, it is more useful to look for a system of choices than one repeated visual gesture. Uses material, surface and construction as carriers of architectural image while rethinking building types through specific contexts and existing fabric. Similarity between projects may therefore lie in research procedure, team organisation, material attitude or sequence of work rather than identical form. VANSMITHLAB records authorship through mechanism rather than superficial resemblance.

## 6. Influence on the discipline

Changed contemporary attitudes to the building envelope by making facade a material and structural argument rather than packaging. Influence becomes visible when questions and working methods enter the practice of other authors, schools, clients or institutions. This does not necessarily mean direct imitation; more often the range of acceptable solutions and the professional vocabulary itself changes.[^clm_impact]

## 7. Critical distance

Architectural fame often concentrates on the photographable image of a building. The stronger test is how concept works with programme, structure, material, city and actual use. This matters for Herzog & de Meuron because reputation can turn successful decisions into universal mythology. VANSMITHLAB separates documented fact, interpretation and editorial judgment: influence is recognised without replacing analysis of limits, context and collective authorship.

## 8. Why the organisation matters now

The practice continues international work across architecture, urban design and cultural buildings.[^clm_current] The relevance of the profile is not defined only by current commissions. The practice remains a useful comparison point because it reveals which professional questions survive changing technologies and fashions and which belonged to a specific historical moment.
