## 1. Origins and context

Experimental Jetset was founded in Amsterdam in 1997. Its founders include Marieke Stolk, Erwin Brinkers and Danny van den Dungen. The organisation emerged within a professional context in which tools, markets and cultural expectations were changing the definition of the discipline itself. Experimental Jetset entered that process not as an isolated author but as a collective structure capable of turning a method into repeatable practice.[^clm_origin]

## 2. How the method works

The core principle can be stated simply: treats graphic design as a system of language, print, typography and cultural reference, using constraints and attention to the medium itself. This matters because method defines not an exterior style but a way of making decisions: what to research, which constraints matter, where conflict lies and how form connects to use. The repeatability of that approach is what makes institutional influence visible.[^clm_method]

## 3. John & Paul & Ringo & George: first anchor example

John & Paul & Ringo & George. A simple shirt listing four names became an example of typographic reduction activating collective memory without an image. The example matters as observable evidence of method: the relationship between problem and form can be inspected in a specific object, image, space or system rather than accepted from studio rhetoric.[^clm_case1]

## 4. Whitney Museum identity: second anchor example

Whitney Museum identity. The responsive W system explored variable geometry and presented identity as a set of rules rather than a fixed logo. The second example tests whether the approach remains coherent when scale, client, medium and cultural context change. It is therefore evidence of a working system rather than an addition to a list of famous projects.[^clm_case2]

## 5. A system rather than a single device

With Experimental Jetset, it is more useful to look for a system of choices than one repeated visual gesture. Treats graphic design as a system of language, print, typography and cultural reference, using constraints and attention to the medium itself. Similarity between projects may therefore lie in research procedure, team organisation, material attitude or sequence of work rather than identical form. VANSMITHLAB records authorship through mechanism rather than superficial resemblance.

## 6. Influence on the discipline

Made a consistent modernist method part of a contemporary debate about neutrality, authorship and cultural memory. Influence becomes visible when questions and working methods enter the practice of other authors, schools, clients or institutions. This does not necessarily mean direct imitation; more often the range of acceptable solutions and the professional vocabulary itself changes.[^clm_impact]

## 7. Critical distance

A recognisable signature is useful until it becomes a formula repeated regardless of context. The real question is whether the method can change with the problem. This matters for Experimental Jetset because reputation can turn successful decisions into universal mythology. VANSMITHLAB separates documented fact, interpretation and editorial judgment: influence is recognised without replacing analysis of limits, context and collective authorship.

## 8. Why the organisation matters now

The studio continues to work in Amsterdam and maintains a detailed public archive of its practice.[^clm_current] The relevance of the profile is not defined only by current commissions. The practice remains a useful comparison point because it reveals which professional questions survive changing technologies and fashions and which belonged to a specific historical moment.
