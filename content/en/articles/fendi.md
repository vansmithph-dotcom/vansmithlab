# Fendi: fur, family, and the longest authorship in fashion

*A Roman workshop where one appointed designer worked for over half a century and turned fur craft into a laboratory.*

## Editorial thesis

Fendi matters as a case of an exceptionally long authorial cycle: fifty-four years under one leadership makes it possible to see what happens to a house when the author does not change.

## Reader question

What does half a century of continuous authorship give a house, and what is it paid for with?

## Short answer

It yields accumulated technique and a complete absence of ruptures, but makes succession extremely difficult. Founded 1925 by Adele Casagrande and Edoardo Fendi; context Rome.

## 1. Origin: a fur and leather workshop

The house began as a Roman workshop working in fur and leather. This is a craft rather than a design foundation, and it explains why the technical base proved more durable than any stylistic decisions.

## 2. A family management structure

Management passed to the founders’ five daughters, each responsible for a separate area. Distributed family leadership is a rare construction in the industry, providing continuity without a single heir.

## 3. Bringing in an outside author

In the mid-1960s the house engaged a young designer who stayed for fifty-four years. The decision to hire an outside author while retaining family ownership proved decisive for everything that followed.

## 4. The double monogram

The house mark was designed by that same author in his first year and remained unchanged for decades. It neither illustrates the product nor refers to history — it is a purely geometric mark, usable on any carrier.

## 5. Fur as an experimental material

The author’s key contribution was treating fur not as a precious material but as ordinary textile. It was shorn, dyed, perforated, combined with other materials, and lightened — contradicting the entire preceding tradition.

## 6. Removing the status function

Devaluing fur as a symbol of wealth was a substantive gesture: the material stopped being a display of means and became a technical problem. It is a rare example of deliberately lowering a material’s status.

## 7. The technical constraints of fur

Fur sets strict rules: cutting depends on nap direction, a pelt has a finite size, joining requires a particular seam. All the house’s experiments read as work with precisely these constraints.

## 8. The bag as a second carrier

A model from the end of the 1990s, designed by a member of the family, introduced a small bag carried under the arm. It codified a category that later became an industry standard.

## 9. The bag as a collectable object

Releasing many versions of one model in different materials and techniques turned it into a collectable. This moved the bag from accessory to an independent line with its own economics.

## 10. Entering a conglomerate

Control passed to a major French group at the turn of the century while family members retained leading roles. Combining corporate ownership with family presence is an unusual and functioning configuration.

## 11. Headquarters as statement

The house took over a 1930s modernist building in Rome, a spatial assertion of belonging to the city and its history. The choice of address works here as a choice of position.

## 12. Entering haute couture

The house acquired a couture line of its own, allowing its craft base to be presented directly. For a workshop with a fur origin this is a logical step: couture is measured in hours of hand work.

## 13. Succession after a long author

The author’s death at the end of the 2010s posed the problem of succession after fifty-four years of continuity. The house divided its areas among several leaders — a risk-reduction strategy rather than a search for a replacement.

## 14. The current appointment

Maria Grazia Chiuri has been named chief creative officer, with a first collection scheduled for the autumn-winter 2026–2027 season. Bringing in an author experienced in leading a major house reads as a bet on institutional stability.

## 15. Critical questions

The critique is justified and serious: the house’s fur foundation is the subject of sustained ethical objection, and the industry as a whole is reducing use of the material. Half a century of dependence on one author also deferred the development of a transferable method by decades.

## 16. How to read this house today

Look at the technical base rather than the mark. The house’s durability rests on its workshop and accumulated material techniques; the monogram and the bag are what that base managed to present to the market.
