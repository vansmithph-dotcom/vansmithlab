## 1. Origins and context

Fuseproject was founded in San Francisco in 1999. Its founders include Yves Béhar. The organisation emerged within a professional context in which tools, markets and cultural expectations were changing the definition of the discipline itself. Fuseproject entered that process not as an isolated author but as a collective structure capable of turning a method into repeatable practice.[^clm_origin]

## 2. How the method works

The core principle can be stated simply: connects industrial design, engineering, brand and entrepreneurship, aiming to carry ideas into manufacturable products and coherent user experiences. This matters because method defines not an exterior style but a way of making decisions: what to research, which constraints matter, where conflict lies and how form connects to use. The repeatability of that approach is what makes institutional influence visible.[^clm_method]

## 3. One Laptop per Child XO: first anchor example

One Laptop per Child XO. The educational laptop was treated not only as technology but as a child-centred object with particular demands for durability, power and identity. The example matters as observable evidence of method: the relationship between problem and form can be inspected in a specific object, image, space or system rather than accepted from studio rhetoric.[^clm_case1]

## 4. SAYL Chair: second anchor example

SAYL Chair. The Herman Miller chair used suspension-bridge logic to reduce back material and make structure the primary visual principle. The second example tests whether the approach remains coherent when scale, client, medium and cultural context change. It is therefore evidence of a working system rather than an addition to a list of famous projects.[^clm_case2]

## 5. A system rather than a single device

With Fuseproject, it is more useful to look for a system of choices than one repeated visual gesture. Connects industrial design, engineering, brand and entrepreneurship, aiming to carry ideas into manufacturable products and coherent user experiences. Similarity between projects may therefore lie in research procedure, team organisation, material attitude or sequence of work rather than identical form. VANSMITHLAB records authorship through mechanism rather than superficial resemblance.

## 6. Influence on the discipline

Helped establish the industrial designer as a participant in product and brand strategy rather than only an author of exterior form. Influence becomes visible when questions and working methods enter the practice of other authors, schools, clients or institutions. This does not necessarily mean direct imitation; more often the range of acceptable solutions and the professional vocabulary itself changes.[^clm_impact]

## 7. Critical distance

A product cannot be judged from a prototype photograph alone. Its design is revealed by manufacturing, cost, repair, longevity, ergonomics and how it ages in use. This matters for Fuseproject because reputation can turn successful decisions into universal mythology. VANSMITHLAB separates documented fact, interpretation and editorial judgment: influence is recognised without replacing analysis of limits, context and collective authorship.

## 8. Why the organisation matters now

Fuseproject continues an independent practice across products, engineering, brands and new ventures.[^clm_current] The relevance of the profile is not defined only by current commissions. The practice remains a useful comparison point because it reveals which professional questions survive changing technologies and fashions and which belonged to a specific historical moment.
