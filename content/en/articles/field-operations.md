## 1. Origins and context

Field Operations was founded in New York in 1998. Its founders include James Corner. The organisation emerged within a professional context in which tools, markets and cultural expectations were changing the definition of the discipline itself. Field Operations entered that process not as an isolated author but as a collective structure capable of turning a method into repeatable practice.[^clm_origin]

## 2. How the method works

The core principle can be stated simply: treats landscape as infrastructure capable of changing neighbourhood economics, ecology, movement and urban image simultaneously. This matters because method defines not an exterior style but a way of making decisions: what to research, which constraints matter, where conflict lies and how form connects to use. The repeatability of that approach is what makes institutional influence visible.[^clm_method]

## 3. The High Line: first anchor example

The High Line. The conversion of an elevated railway into a park became one of the most influential examples of urban infrastructure reuse. The example matters as observable evidence of method: the relationship between problem and form can be inspected in a specific object, image, space or system rather than accepted from studio rhetoric.[^clm_case1]

## 4. Freshkills Park: second anchor example

Freshkills Park. The long-term transformation of a former landfill into a vast landscape shows design as a decades-long process of ecological recovery. The second example tests whether the approach remains coherent when scale, client, medium and cultural context change. It is therefore evidence of a working system rather than an addition to a list of famous projects.[^clm_case2]

## 5. A system rather than a single device

With Field Operations, it is more useful to look for a system of choices than one repeated visual gesture. Treats landscape as infrastructure capable of changing neighbourhood economics, ecology, movement and urban image simultaneously. Similarity between projects may therefore lie in research procedure, team organisation, material attitude or sequence of work rather than identical form. VANSMITHLAB records authorship through mechanism rather than superficial resemblance.

## 6. Influence on the discipline

Changed perceptions of landscape architecture from decorative support to a central instrument of urban transformation. Influence becomes visible when questions and working methods enter the practice of other authors, schools, clients or institutions. This does not necessarily mean direct imitation; more often the range of acceptable solutions and the professional vocabulary itself changes.[^clm_impact]

## 7. Critical distance

Landscape unfolds over time, so opening-day appearance says little about long-term quality. Growth, maintenance, climate resilience, access and actual public use matter. This matters for Field Operations because reputation can turn successful decisions into universal mythology. VANSMITHLAB separates documented fact, interpretation and editorial judgment: influence is recognised without replacing analysis of limits, context and collective authorship.

## 8. Why the organisation matters now

Field Operations continues international work on public spaces, parks and urban systems.[^clm_current] The relevance of the profile is not defined only by current commissions. The practice remains a useful comparison point because it reveals which professional questions survive changing technologies and fashions and which belonged to a specific historical moment.
