## 10. Form and geometry

The geometry of The Museum — Route, Display, and the Management of Attention organises not only silhouette but relations among zones, routes, furniture, and sightlines. Plan and section are best read as diagrams of action.

## 11. Surface, light, and colour

Light and surface make time visible. Neutral surfaces, cases, glass, stone, timber, museum lighting, climate control, and security systems reflect and absorb light differently, while traces of touch and wear transform space from image into material experience.

## 12. Tactility, sound, and movement

Tactility appears in handles, rails, textiles, floor surfaces, and furniture. Human movement creates the real montage of space: turn, pause, approach, and distance become part of the design.

## 13. Production system

Behind the visible interior lies a production system: design, working drawings, fabrication, installation, equipment supply, and maintenance. It is essential to distinguish authored form from the infrastructure that makes it operational.

## 14. Tools and infrastructure

Infrastructure includes services, lighting, climate, storage, safety, logistics, and maintenance. The calmer a space looks, the more complex the hidden coordination of these systems may be.

## 15. Mass dissemination

Dissemination occurred through publications, exhibitions, photography, repetition of techniques, and professional education. The canon was therefore produced not only by buildings but by images that taught generations how to recognise them.

## 16. Professional practice

For practitioners, The Museum — Route, Display, and the Management of Attention is useful as a methodological case: it requires simultaneous attention to scenario, body scale, materials, operation, economics, and visual identity.

## 17. Connection to architecture

The architectural connection is direct: space establishes boundaries, sequences, light, and construction. It links detail to city or landscape and makes typology part of a larger system.

## 18. Connection to object design

Object design participates through furniture, hardware, lighting, equipment, and display systems. In strong examples, objects are not added after architecture; they refine its scale and mode of use.

## 19. Connection to fashion

The link to fashion comes through rituals of display, the body, seasonality, and the production of desire. It is especially clear in nodes such as выставка; куратор; коллекция; Neue Nationalgalerie; MoMA.

## 20. Connection to image-making

Images alter the fate of space: photography selects a viewpoint, magazines impose sequence, advertising turns details into signs. A canonical image may become better known than the everyday experience of an actual visitor.

## 21. Connection to technology

Technology is present both as hidden infrastructure and visible language. It shapes calculation, fabrication, climate, light, security, media surfaces, and the ways a space can be updated.

## 22. Connection to materials

Materials—neutral surfaces, cases, glass, stone, timber, museum lighting, climate control, and security systems—should be read as systems of properties: mass, reflection, ageing, repair, availability, and tactility. Material becomes an argument only when its properties support the scenario.

## 23. Institutions and canon

The canon is formed by museums, archives, professional publications, heritage bodies, and education. For historic sites, restoration is also interpretive: it reveals which version of the past an institution chooses to preserve.

## 24. Market and commercial logic

Commercial logic affects area, density, durability, refresh cycles, and staff work. Even non-commercial spaces operate within budgets, maintenance schedules, and institutional constraints.

## 25. Critique and limitations

The main limitation is the temptation to turn a strong spatial image into a universal recipe. A technique convincing in one climate, scale, or programme can become inconvenient and expensive when copied mechanically.

## 26. Errors and myths

A common error is to read the subject only through photographs and treat visual cleanliness as proof of functionality. Real evaluation requires plan, section, route, detail, maintenance, and evidence of use.

## 27. Sustainability, durability, and maintenance

Durability depends not only on the strength of neutral surfaces, cases, glass, stone, timber, museum lighting, climate control, and security systems, but on repairability, replacement of wearing parts, and adaptation of services. Preserving a valuable space often means controlled change rather than freezing it.

## 28. Contemporary condition

Today the subject exists simultaneously as a working typology, a historical canon, and a source of new hybrids. Digital booking, screens, sensors, accessibility requirements, and climate expectations alter scenarios without eliminating the basic work of route and scale.

## 29. What the topic changed in the discipline

Its major disciplinary effect is the understanding of space as a connected system rather than a collection of rooms and attractive surfaces. The Museum — Route, Display, and the Management of Attention shows that architecture, objects, images, technology, and use can be designed as one whole.

## 30. How to read the topic today

Today The Museum — Route, Display, and the Management of Attention is best read in four passes: first route and scale, then materials and light, then infrastructure and economics, and only then canonical photographs. This order restores the project’s real complexity.
