# Ulm Stool

*An object as a tool: furniture, education and systems thinking*

## Editorial thesis

Ulm Stool matters to VANSMITHLAB not because it is called an icon, but because one object reveals the chain author → material → production → space → image → canon. An object as a tool: furniture, education and systems thinking.

## Reader question

Why did Ulm Stool become important to design history if it is only one piece of furniture?

## Short answer

Because Ulm Stool makes a new relationship between form, body, material and production visible. Its historical role lies in the design problem it formulated and the way that formulation outlived its original moment.

## 1. Definition and boundaries of the object

The Ulm Stool is a compact wooden stool designed by Max Bill, Hans Gugelot and Paul Hildinger for the Ulm School of Design in 1954. It can act as a seat, low table, support, portable container or storage element.

## 2. Origins and prehistory

The object grew out of the young school’s economic and pedagogical conditions, including chronic underfunding. A cheap, light and multifunctional object was needed for students and teachers moving between classrooms, accommodation, refectory and terrace.
