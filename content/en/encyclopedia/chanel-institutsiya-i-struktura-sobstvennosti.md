# Chanel (House of Chanel)

**Chanel** — a French haute couture and luxury goods house, founded in Paris in 1910 by Gabrielle "Coco" Chanel. Today it is one of the few major couture houses to remain a private company, wholly owned by the Wertheimer family.

## 1. The founding of the House (1909–1910)

Gabrielle Chanel opened her first millinery workshop at 160 Boulevard Malesherbes in 1909 — on the ground floor of the Parisian apartment of Étienne Balsan. In 1910 she officially registered the business under the name "Chanel Modes" and opened a boutique at 21 rue Cambon in Paris — an address that remains the historic heart of the House to this day. It is the year 1910 that the House of Chanel officially states as its date of foundation.

From hats the business quickly expanded to clothing: jersey dresses, simple coats and garments that allowed women to move without the corset and heavy trimmings characteristic of Belle Époque fashion. The initial financial support was provided by Arthur "Boy" Capel, an English entrepreneur and Chanel's lover.

## 2. The formation of a signature language (the 1920s)

- **1921** — on 5 May the perfume **Chanel No. 5** was presented, created by the French-Russian perfumer Ernest Beaux. According to legend, Chanel chose sample number five from a series of trials numbered 1–5 and 20–24 and linked the number to her luck (the House's collection shows were traditionally held on 5 May — the fifth day of the fifth month).
- **1924** — Pierre Wertheimer and Théophile Bader (co-owner of Galeries Lafayette) founded the company Parfums Chanel for the production and worldwide distribution of the fragrance: the Wertheimers received a 70% stake, Bader 20%, and Chanel herself 10% with royalties. This deal determined the structure of the business's ownership for decades and subsequently became the source of a long conflict between Chanel and the Wertheimer family for control over the profitable perfume line.
- **1925** — the signature monogram logo of two interlaced "C" letters was formalised.
- **1926** — the "little black dress" (la petite robe noire) was presented: American Vogue compared the pared-down black dress to a "Ford" — an object accessible and suitable to women of any circle.

## 3. The wartime period and a contested page of history (1939–1945)

At the start of the Second World War Chanel closed the atelier on the rue Cambon (the perfume boutique, however, continued its work), and she herself moved into the Hôtel Ritz in German-occupied Paris. During this period she began a relationship with Hans Günther von Dincklage, a German military intelligence (Abwehr) officer.

Historians differ in their assessment of the degree and nature of Chanel's collaboration with the German authorities. It is documented that she attempted to use Nazi "Aryan" laws to remove her Jewish partners — the Wertheimer brothers — from the ownership of the perfume business; the attempt was not successful, since the Wertheimers had in advance transferred formal control of the company to a person of non-Aryan origin. According to data from declassified French archives (made public in 2014), Chanel was also registered by German intelligence under the agent name "Westminster" and took part in an unsuccessful diplomatic mission to Madrid in 1943–1944. After the liberation of Paris in 1944 Chanel was summoned for questioning by the French purge committee, but no charges were brought against her; she soon left for Switzerland, where she spent a large part of the subsequent decade and a half.

## 4. The return and the golden classics (1954–1971)

In 1954, at the age of 71, Chanel presented her first collection after a fifteen-year break, returning to fashion with a philosophy directly opposite to Christian Dior's exuberant "New Look": practicality, a minimum of construction, freedom of movement. The French press met the show coolly, the American with enthusiasm, which ensured the House's second commercial flight.

From this period come the two most recognisable objects in the history of fashion:

- **The tweed suit** (presented in 1954) — a collarless straight-cut jacket with patch pockets and a knee-length skirt, sewn in tweed and weighted along the inner edge with a chain for a correct fit.
- **The 2.55 bag** (February 1955, hence the name) — a quilted shoulder bag on a chain, for the first time allowing women to carry a bag without occupying their hands; the quilting was inspired by the jockey jackets Chanel saw at the races.

Coco Chanel died on 10 January 1971 at the age of 87 in her apartments at the Hôtel Ritz in Paris.

## 5. Between Chanel and Lagerfeld (1971–1983)

After the founder's death the House was led successively by several designers — including Gaston Berthelot — whose period the industry as a whole characterises as commercially and creatively less convincing compared with the subsequent era.

## 6. The Karl Lagerfeld era (1983–2019)

In 1983 Karl Lagerfeld became the artistic director of all directions — haute couture, prêt-à-porter and accessories. Over 36 years of work he preserved the House's key codes (tweed, quilting, pearls, the black-and-white palette, the CC monogram) but radically widened their visual and commercial language: he turned shows into large-scale productions, brought the 2.55 bag to the status of a cult object of mass demand, replaced the historical "Mademoiselle" clasp with the CC monogram, experimented with materials (denim, neoprene, metallic) and lifted the House to the rank of one of the most recognisable luxury brands in the world. Lagerfeld led the House until his death in February 2019.

## 7. Virginie Viard (2019–2024)

Lagerfeld's successor was Virginie Viard — his long-time right hand, who had worked in the Chanel studio since 1987. She led the creative direction from 2019 to June 2024, when she announced her departure from the House; the following collections, until the appointment of a new director, were produced by an internal studio team.

## 8. Matthieu Blazy and a new chapter (from 2024/2025)

On 12 December 2024 the House announced the appointment of **Matthieu Blazy**, previously the creative director of Bottega Veneta, as the new artistic director of Chanel's fashion directions — the fourth in the company's 114-year history. Blazy began work in April 2025 and presented his debut ready-to-wear spring–summer 2026 collection at Paris Fashion Week on 6 October 2025 at the Grand Palais; the show was built as a dialogue with the founder's heritage and ended with a standing ovation. Later, in January 2026, he presented his first haute couture collection.

## 9. Ownership structure and current indicators

Chanel remains a private company, unlisted, controlled since 1954 by the Wertheimer family (descendants of Pierre Wertheimer); the co-owners are the brothers Alain and Gérard Wertheimer. Since 2018 the group's head office has officially been located in London. The global CEO since January 2022 has been Leena Nair — the first woman and the first person of Indian origin in this position, previously the chief human resources officer of Unilever.

According to the company's official financial results:

- Revenue for 2023 was **US$19.7 billion** (growth of 16% on a comparable basis), operating profit US$6.4 billion.
- Revenue for 2024 fell to **US$18.7 billion** (−4.3% on a comparable basis) against the background of a difficult macroeconomic conjuncture; operating profit was US$4.48 billion, while capital expenditure grew by 43% to US$1.76 billion, which the company explained by investment in the retail network and production.

## 10. Cultural significance

Chanel is the only twentieth-century couturier included by Time magazine in the list of the hundred most influential people of the century. The tweed suit, the 2.55 bag, the No. 5 fragrance and the CC monogram remain among the most recognisable objects in the history of fashion and continue to be reissued and reinterpreted by each successive creative director of the House.
