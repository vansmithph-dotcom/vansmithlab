## Editorial thesis

Eames Lounge Chair matters to VANSMITHLAB not because it is called an icon, but because one object reveals the chain author → material → production → space → image → canon. Moulded plywood, comfort and postwar luxury.

## Reader question

Why did Eames Lounge Chair become important to design history if it is only one piece of furniture?

## Short answer

Because Eames Lounge Chair makes a new relationship between form, body, material and production visible. Its historical role lies in the design problem it formulated and the way that formulation outlived its original moment.

## 1. Definition and boundaries of the object

The Eames Lounge Chair and Ottoman is a reclining chair with separate footrest, introduced by Herman Miller in 1956 from a design by Charles and Ray Eames. It combines three moulded plywood shells, removable cushions, a cast metal base and concealed resilient connections.

## 2. Origins and prehistory

The project grew from the Eameses’ long experimentation with moulded plywood beginning in the 1940s. Unlike their more democratic earlier seating, the new model consciously revisited the English club chair and translated its comfort into industrial modernism.
