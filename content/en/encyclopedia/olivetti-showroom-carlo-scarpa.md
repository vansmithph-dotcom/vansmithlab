## 21. Connection to technology

Technology is present both as hidden infrastructure and visible language. It shapes calculation, fabrication, climate, light, security, media surfaces, and the ways a space can be updated.

Materials—stone, mosaic, metal, glass, timber, plaster, and water—should be read as systems of properties: mass, reflection, ageing, repair, availability, and tactility. Material becomes an argument only when its properties support the scenario.

## 23. Institutions and canon

The canon is formed by museums, archives, professional publications, heritage bodies, and education. For historic sites, restoration is also interpretive: it reveals which version of the past an institution chooses to preserve.

## 24. Market and commercial logic

Commercial logic affects area, density, durability, refresh cycles, and staff work. Even non-commercial spaces operate within budgets, maintenance schedules, and institutional constraints.

## 25. Critique and limitations

The main limitation is the temptation to turn a strong spatial image into a universal recipe. A technique convincing in one climate, scale, or programme can become inconvenient and expensive when copied mechanically.

## 26. Errors and myths

A common error is to read the subject only through photographs and treat visual cleanliness as proof of functionality. Real evaluation requires plan, section, route, detail, maintenance, and evidence of use.

## 27. Sustainability, durability, and maintenance

Durability depends not only on the strength of stone, mosaic, metal, glass, timber, plaster, and water, but on repairability, replacement of wearing parts, and adaptation of services. Preserving a valuable space often means controlled change rather than freezing it.

## 28. Contemporary condition

Today the subject exists simultaneously as a working typology, a historical canon, and a source of new hybrids. Digital booking, screens, sensors, accessibility requirements, and climate expectations alter scenarios without eliminating the basic work of route and scale.

## 29. What the topic changed in the discipline

Its major disciplinary effect is the understanding of space as a connected system rather than a collection of rooms and attractive surfaces. Olivetti Showroom / Carlo Scarpa shows that architecture, objects, images, technology, and use can be designed as one whole.

## 30. How to read the topic today

Today Olivetti Showroom / Carlo Scarpa is best read in four passes: first route and scale, then materials and light, then infrastructure and economics, and only then canonical photographs. This order restores the project’s real complexity.
