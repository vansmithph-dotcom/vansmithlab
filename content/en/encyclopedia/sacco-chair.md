# Sacco

*The anti-chair: free posture and unfixed form*

## Editorial thesis

Sacco matters to VANSMITHLAB not because it is called an icon, but because one object reveals the chain author → material → production → space → image → canon. The anti-chair: free posture and unfixed form.

## Reader question

Why did Sacco become important to design history if it is only one piece of furniture?

## Short answer

Because Sacco makes a new relationship between form, body, material and production visible. Its historical role lies in the design problem it formulated and the way that formulation outlived its original moment.

## 1. Definition and boundaries of the object

Sacco is a frameless beanbag chair designed by Piero Gatti, Cesare Paolini and Franco Teodoro in 1968 for Zanotta. A flexible envelope filled with expanded-polystyrene pellets has no single predetermined shape; it changes under the user’s body.

## 2. Origins and prehistory

The project negated the conventional chair of legs, frame, seat and back. Instead of fixed ergonomic geometry, the authors proposed a volume of small mobile elements that takes form only in use.

## 16. Professional practice

For professional designers, Sacco is useful as a methodological case. Sacco shifts the question from what is the ideal chair shape to can a chair have no final shape at all. The user becomes co-author of each temporary configuration. The practical value lies in seeing where form follows material and production and where it becomes cultural statement.

## 26. Mistakes and myths

The beanbag can look like an absence of design, but simplicity depends on careful choice of volume, pellet behaviour, textile and envelope. Eliminating the frame is a design decision, not the elimination of design.

## 27. Sustainability, longevity and maintenance

Long service is possible through cover repair and replacement or topping-up of filling, while expanded polystyrene raises problems around small plastic particles and end-of-life handling. Environmental assessment must include leakage, refilling and disposal.
