## Editorial thesis

LC4 matters to VANSMITHLAB not because it is called an icon, but because one object reveals the chain author → material → production → space → image → canon. The body as a mechanism of rest: adjustable posture, tubular steel and a new ergonomics.

## Reader question

Why did LC4 become important to design history if it is only one piece of furniture?

## Short answer

Because LC4 makes a new relationship between form, body, material and production visible. Its historical role lies in the design problem it formulated and the way that formulation outlived its original moment.

## 1. Definition and boundaries of the object

LC4 is an adjustable chaise longue designed by Le Corbusier, Pierre Jeanneret and Charlotte Perriand in 1928. Its upper frame can shift on the base, allowing the body to move from a near-seated to a reclined posture.

## 2. Origins and prehistory

The project emerged while the collaborators investigated equipment for living and replaced historical furniture types with clear objects for modern life. Perriand brought a particularly strong concern for the body, metal construction and actual use.
