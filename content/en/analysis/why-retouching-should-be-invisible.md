# Why retouching should be invisible

*Good retouching does not decorate the frame. It returns trust to it.*

## Editorial thesis

Retouching becomes a problem not when it exists, but when the viewer starts seeing it instead of the photograph. Invisible retouching preserves texture, age, material and the plausibility of light.

## Reader question

Why can a photograph feel strong before we understand its plot?

## Short answer

Because photography works not only through what it depicts, but through a system of relations: light, color, distance, background, material, pause and visual trust assemble faster than meaning.

## 1. Defect and surface

A pimple can be removed. Skin should not be turned into plastic.

The temporary is removed, the permanent kept. A spot is an event, skin texture a property, and they cannot be treated alike.

## 2. Dust and scale

Dust in product photography can be removed. But when every micro-trace disappears, the object loses scale and life.

Removing every micro-trace deprives an object of size. It is small imperfections that tell a viewer how large a thing is.

## 3. Interference versus reality

Retouching should often remove technical interference, not change reality for smoothness.

Retouching removes what obstructed the shoot rather than what was disliked about the subject. Distinguishing these two tasks is the profession.

## 4. Local correction and overall light

The main mistake is retouching locally while forgetting the light of the whole image.

Correcting an area without regard to the light of the whole frame produces a mismatch a viewer notices without being able to name it.

## 5. Agreement with the original light

If the shadow was soft, skin cannot keep hard blotches. If the light was hard, the face cannot become porcelain.

Soft shadow permits no hard blotches on skin; hard light permits no porcelain smoothness. Post must obey the shoot.

## 6. Fabric is not rubbish

In fashion, retouching must respect fabric: folds, seams and texture are not trash.

Folds, seams, and weave carry information about construction. Removing them erases the pattern cutter’s work along with the defect.

## 7. Pores and plausibility

In portraiture, vanished pores often look worse than real imperfections.

A vanished pore looks worse than a visible irregularity, because it violates the expectation of skin. A viewer does not analyse but stops believing.

## 8. Cleanliness without loss of material

In product photography, retouching should clean the frame without killing material.

In product photography retouching removes dirt and leaves texture. The boundary lies in whether a mark carries information about the surface.

## 9. The mark of good retouching

Good retouching is not recognized by smoothness, but by the fact that the photograph still breathes.

Quality is recognised not by smoothness but by the image continuing to breathe. It is the only working test.

## 10. An ethics of taste

Invisible retouching is an ethics of taste: remove the unnecessary without stealing reality.

Removing the superfluous without stealing reality is a formulation that reduces to no rule and therefore demands judgement in every frame.
