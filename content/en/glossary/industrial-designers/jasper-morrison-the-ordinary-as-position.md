# Jasper Morrison

*Refusing expressiveness as a deliberate professional position rather than as modesty.*

## Editorial thesis

Morrison matters for turning ordinariness from an absence of quality into a verifiable criterion: a thing is judged by how it behaves in its tenth year of use.

## Reader question

What is left to a designer who gives up the expressive gesture?

## Short answer

Proportion, material, weight, and behaviour over time — everything verified by use rather than by looking.

## 1. Position

Morrison works as an industrial designer and consistently takes a position against spectacle. His objects are hard to describe in words precisely because there is nothing in them to describe except quality of execution.

## 2. Ordinariness as a choice

Ordinariness here is not a consequence of caution. It is a chosen constraint: by giving up a recognisable device, the author denies himself an easy way to stand out and must work through precision.

## 3. Super Normal

Together with Naoto Fukasawa he formulated the notion of the super normal and presented it as an exhibition. The selected objects are mostly anonymous, and this is part of the argument: quality does not require authorship.

## 4. Selection as authorial work

The project’s curatorial component showed that selection is a form of design. Deciding what deserves to be shown is more substantive than many decisions about form.

## 5. Early work and hard simplicity

His first known objects of the 1980s were built on straight lines and a minimum of elements. The principle was stated there already: construction presented without commentary.

## 6. Work with mass production

A substantial part of his practice involves large furniture and household manufacturers. A position of ordinariness requires an edition: a one-off is not tested by everyday life.

## 7. Material as principal means

When form is reduced to the simple, difference moves to material and finish. The same silhouette in beech, polypropylene, and aluminium yields three different objects.

## 8. Proportion as the site of work

Without ornament, the ratio of dimensions becomes decisive. An error of proportion here is covered by nothing and is therefore immediately visible — the most demanding mode of work.

## 9. Atmosphere as a working notion

He describes a project’s goal through the feel of a room as a whole rather than of an object. A thing is judged by its contribution to an environment, and that contribution can be negative.

## 10. Attitude to novelty

Novelty is not treated as a value in itself. An object that repeats a known solution better than its predecessor is accepted as a legitimate design outcome.

## 11. Against the authorial signature

A recognisable hand is treated as an obstruction: it makes an object a statement about its author rather than a tool for its owner. This is the direct opposite of the star-designer model.

## 12. Publishing work

He consistently publishes texts and books about his approach. This matters: a position not put into words is indistinguishable from having none.

## 13. Link to the European tradition

The method continues a line running from mid-century product discipline: restricted palette, priority of clarity, rejection of the superfluous. The difference is that there is no rule set here, only judgement.

## 14. Difference from the Japanese line

Unlike an approach proceeding from behavioural observation, the starting point here is the object itself and its history. The two positions converge in result and diverge in reasoning.

## 15. What transfers

The criterion transfers: judge an object by its behaviour over time rather than by first impression. This is a verifiable requirement applicable to any category.

## 16. What does not transfer

Taste does not transfer. The difference between successful simplicity and poverty is not describable by rule, and here the method reaches the limit of authorial competence.

## 17. Critique

A justified objection: a position of ordinariness is available to an author with an established reputation. For a beginner it reads as an absence of ideas, and it is therefore not a neutral professional strategy.

## 18. A second objection

The restrained objects of this circle are expensive. Ordinariness in execution requires precision that must be paid for, and the promise of accessibility is only partly kept.

## 19. Link to the knowledge base

The material connects to the entries on minimalism, on the super normal, on product systems, and on why absence can cost more than decoration.

## 20. How to read this today

When assessing such an object, look not for a device but for wear: how an edge behaves, whether a joint loosens, whether a surface ages well. Here that is the content of the work.
