# Philippe Starck

*The designer as a public figure and media argument, selling an object together with their own name.*

## Editorial thesis

Starck matters as a case in which an author’s name became a production asset: a designer’s recognisability began selling an object as effectively as its quality.

## Reader question

What happens to an object when its author’s name is better known than the object itself?

## Short answer

The object begins doing two jobs at once: serving use and confirming its owner’s membership of the circle that knows the name.

## 1. Position

Starck works as a designer across an exceptionally wide range: from household object to interior, hotel, and vehicle. The absence of specialisation is part of his position.

## 2. Democratic design as declaration

He consistently frames the task as raising the quality of affordable objects. That rhetoric preceded mass collaborations and largely justified them.

## 3. The squeezer as an object of dispute

His most-discussed object is an aluminium citrus squeezer of the early 1990s whose form recalls an insect. It works worse than an ordinary one and sells better — and that is the substance of the case.

## 4. Function versus presence

The object is presented as something to look at rather than to use. The author spoke of it as a conversation starter, which honestly describes its actual function.

## 5. Transparent plastic

A chair of the early 2000s, moulded in transparent polycarbonate to the silhouette of a historical armchair, joined citation and technology. A form of the past executed in a material the past did not have.

## 6. Citation as device

Using a recognisable historical silhouette in a new material is a postmodern device. It allows modernity and familiarity to be sold at once.

## 7. The hotel as genre

A substantial part of his practice is hotel interiors, where the environment works as a set for a stay. Here his method fits: the task is precisely to produce an impression.

## 8. Mass collaborations

He worked with large manufacturers of inexpensive goods, carrying an authorial name into the mass segment. This is an early and influential model, later repeated by many.

## 9. The name as asset

The presence of a recognisable name raises price and press attention independently of a product’s properties. He used this mechanism earlier and more consistently than most colleagues.

## 10. The public figure

He deliberately built a media presence: interviews, television, provocative formulations. The designer became a character, and this changed the profession as much as his objects did.

## 11. Speed and volume

His catalogue is unusually large, implying studio work and delegation. Volume is part of the model: the name must be present in many categories at once.

## 12. Attitude to ecology

In a later period he publicly shifted toward a discourse of responsibility and materials. Assessing that shift is complicated by the same thing as everything else: declaration outruns verifiable indicators.

## 13. What transfers

The model transfers: the author as a media figure available to a mass manufacturer. It became an industry norm and is reproduced across every segment.

## 14. What does not transfer

The name effect itself does not transfer. It works for someone already known and is therefore not a strategy available to most.

## 15. Substantive critique

The principal objection is technical: some objects subordinate use to image. A squeezer that is awkward to wash and a chair that is uncomfortable do not perform their stated function.

## 16. A second objection

The rhetoric of accessibility sits badly with the price of much of the range. The democratic promise is kept in individual collaborations and not in the main catalogue.

## 17. The case for the defence

In his favour, he widened the audience for design. People with no interest in objects learned that a thing has an author — and that is a result in itself.

## 18. Historical position

He is a transitional figure between the mid-century designer-engineer and the contemporary designer-brand. His case is usefully read as the boundary between two models of the profession.

## 19. Link to the knowledge base

The material connects to the entries on postmodernism, citation, the store as media, and objects of Italian manufacture.

## 20. How to read this today

Separate two questions: does the object work, and does the statement work. Conflating these assessments makes discussion of him fruitless in both directions.
