# Gaetano Pesce

*Industrial production tuned so that every example in a series differs from the next.*

## Editorial thesis

Pesce matters as an author who contested industry’s central promise — sameness: he built processes in which deviation is the result rather than a defect.

## Reader question

Can serial production yield non-identical objects?

## Short answer

It can, if a variable is deliberately introduced into the process: hand pouring, incomplete control of mixing, permitted deformation. Difference is then produced systematically.

## 1. Position

Pesce worked as a designer and architect and held a consistently anti-functionalist position. For him an object is a statement, political among other things, rather than the solution to a utilitarian problem.

## 2. Against sameness

The central objection of his practice is addressed to the modernist ideal of repeatability. He regarded identical products as an impoverishment rather than an achievement.

## 3. The diversified series

He formulated the notion of a series within which examples differ. This requires not abandoning production but retuning it: a variable is introduced deliberately and under control.

## 4. Resin as material of choice

Polyurethane and epoxy resin permit hand pouring with unpredictable colour mixing. The material was chosen precisely because it resists complete control.

## 5. A chair delivered compressed

His best-known work of the late 1960s is a polyurethane foam chair supplied in vacuum packaging. On opening it expanded to full volume in front of the buyer.

## 6. Unpacking as event

The object’s expansion turned delivery into a performance. It is a rare case of a logistical decision becoming a substantive part of a work.

## 7. The figure and its reading

The form of this chair with its attached sphere reads as a female figure with a ball and chain. The author spoke of it as a statement about the condition of women, and the object was programmatic from the outset.

## 8. The contested statement

Reading the object as a political statement has been disputed: critics noted that the metaphor reproduces what it criticises. That argument remains open and is part of the material.

## 9. Softness as construction

A significant part of his objects has no rigid frame: form is held by the foam itself. This moves structural work into chemistry rather than geometry.

## 10. The felt chair

In the 1980s he produced a chair of dense felt, resin-impregnated in its lower part and left soft above. One material performs two incompatible functions through a gradient of impregnation.

## 11. Imperfection as statement

Runs, uneven edges, and pouring marks are not removed. Preserving them asserts the value of the trace of a hand inside an industrial process.

## 12. Colour as chance

Mixing tinted resins produces an unpredictable pattern. The author sets a range while the specific result is determined by the process — governing probability rather than form.

## 13. Attitude to architecture

His architectural projects continue the same line: facades with irregular rhythm, vegetation as part of the envelope, refusal of the repeating module.

## 14. Production complexity

The manual component limits run size and raises cost. The method scales poorly, and this is not a side effect but a direct consequence of its central principle.

## 15. The Italian context

The practice sits within an Italian tradition in which industrial firms funded radical criticism of their own model. Without that context the work would not have appeared.

## 16. What transfers

The question itself transfers: where in a production process can a controlled variable be permitted. This is a constructive framing applicable well beyond furniture.

## 17. What does not transfer

The economics do not transfer. The model requires manual labour and a high price, and the promise of accessibility present in the rhetoric was not kept in practice.

## 18. Critique

A justified objection: the uniqueness of every example complicates repair, replacement, and valuation. What gains as statement loses as an object of long use.

## 19. Link to the knowledge base

The material connects to the entries on radical design, composites, moulded plastic, and individual objects of the Italian line.

## 20. How to read this today

Compare two examples of one model. The difference between them is the work; a photograph of one example conveys less than half of it.
