# Alexandra Munroe

*Curatorial work that rewrites art history by including what the canon left outside itself.*

## Editorial thesis

Munroe matters for showing that expanding a canon is a research task rather than a representational one — it requires archival work, not a quota.

## Reader question

How is what the Western canon excluded brought into art history?

## Short answer

Through archival work and exhibitions showing connections the canon did not see: not by adding names to a list but by rebuilding the chronology itself.

## 1. Position

Munroe works as a curator and scholar specialising in Asian contemporary art within a major international museum. Her work joins research with institutional strategy.

## 2. The problem of the canon

Western art history of the twentieth century was built on a European and American line. Artistic movements of other regions remained outside the chronology rather than inside it in secondary positions.

## 3. Research as condition

Inclusion requires work with archives, languages, and local history. This is slow scholarly work, without which widening the roster remains decorative.

## 4. The exhibition as rewriting of chronology

Her projects show parallel developments instead of influence radiating from a centre to a periphery. The change of scheme matters more than the number of names included.

## 5. Against the influence model

A scheme in which a region receives Western ideas belatedly describes the observer’s position rather than the facts. The alternative is networks of exchange without a single centre.

## 6. Postwar Japanese art

A substantial part of her work concerns postwar Japanese movements that developed simultaneously with Western ones and independently of them. Chronological simultaneity is the principal argument.

## 7. Acquisitions as long-term work

Changing the composition of a collection acts longer than an exhibition. An acquired work stays in the institution and changes what can be shown in future.

## 8. Institutional strategy

Her position involves working with a board, funding, and museum priorities. A curatorial decision here is inseparable from an administrative one.

## 9. A museum’s international network

Working in an institution with branches in different countries raises the question of whose history is told in each. This is a structural problem of the networked museum.

## 10. The risk of exoticisation

An exhibition of regional art in a Western museum risks presenting it as exotic. Avoiding this is possible only by abandoning a geographic frame for a thematic one.

## 11. The catalogue as long-term result

A scholarly catalogue outlives an exhibition and serves as a source for subsequent research. For canon-widening work it is the principal carrier of the result.

## 12. What transfers

The procedure transfers: archival research, revision of chronology, acquisition, publication. The sequence applies to any excluded material.

## 13. What does not transfer

The institutional resource does not transfer. Work of this scale requires a museum with a budget for research and acquisition.

## 14. Critique

A justified objection: inclusion in a Western museum itself confirms its right to determine significance. Widening the canon strengthens the institution that composed it.

## 15. A second objection

Selection within a region reproduces hierarchies of its own. Replacing one centre with another does not remove the problem of a centre as such.

## 16. The case for the defence

The alternative to inclusion is continued absence. Working from inside an institution is limited but produces a verifiable result in collections and publications.

## 17. Historical position

Her work documents the period when widening the canon moved from demand into institutional practice with a budget and staff.

## 18. Link to the knowledge base

The material connects to the entries on the museum and design history, the exhibition, canonical objects, and the Japanese line.

## 19. How to read this today

Look at the composition of a collection rather than at an exhibition programme. Acquisitions show an institution’s long-term position more accurately than temporary projects.

## 20. A test question

Ask whether the permanent display changed. A temporary exhibition signals intention; a permanent one records the result.
