## Editorial thesis

Claire Wilcox belongs to the generation of curators that helped make fashion one of the central public languages of the Victoria and Albert Museum. Her exhibitions connected archive, designer practice, scenography and broad audiences while demonstrating that museum fashion requires precise work with the body, historical context and authorship.

## Reader question

How can a curator make fashion accessible to a broad audience without turning historical dress into entertainment scenery?

## Short answer

Wilcox worked for many years at the Victoria and Albert Museum while developing fashion curation academically. Radical Fashion, Vivienne Westwood, The Golden Age of Couture, Alexander McQueen: Savage Beauty, Frida Kahlo and Fashioning Masculinities demonstrate a range from designer monograph to social questions of body and gender.

## 1. The V&A: collection as a long conversation

Wilcox spent many years connected to the V&A fashion department, working with the historic collection as an active research resource. [^clm_vsl_cur_005_001]

## 2. Mechanism: collection as system

Long work with a collection lets a curator see objects not only as seasonal exhibits but as nodes linking periods. The point is not an isolated gesture but a way of organising practice. The decision connects author, material, institution, technology and audience, so it cannot be reduced to a personal signature or a polished result.
