# Zoë Ryan

*Curating architecture and design as an institution’s programmatic work rather than as a series of individual exhibitions.*

## Editorial thesis

Ryan matters as a figure working with design inside museum and university systems: her case shows what the institutionalisation of a discipline requires.

## Reader question

What is required for design to occupy a stable place in an institution’s programme?

## Short answer

Staff, an acquisition budget, and a repeatable programme: a one-off exhibition does not create a position; continuity does.

## 1. Position

Ryan works as a curator of architecture and design and has directed institutions in the field. Her practice joins exhibition work with administration.

## 2. Design inside an art museum

A design department in an art museum always occupies a subordinate position in budget and floor area. Half a curator’s task here is defending that position.

## 3. A programme instead of exhibitions

Stability is secured by a repeatable format: a series, a biennial, a regular cycle. An individual project creates no expectation in an audience.

## 4. Contemporary design as a difficult subject

Showing contemporary practice is harder than historical: works are unfinished, authors alive, assessment unsettled. The institution assumes the risk of judgement.

## 5. Commissioning as a curatorial instrument

Commissioning new work instead of displaying existing work changes the curator’s role: they participate in production rather than selection. This is closer to editorial work.

## 6. Acquisition as a long-term decision

Including an object in a collection acts longer than an exhibition and determines what a museum can show in twenty years. It is the most consequential part of the work.

## 7. The problem of conserving design

Design objects age differently from painting: plastic degrades, electronics obsolesce, textile deteriorates. Conservation here requires decisions an art museum does not usually make.

## 8. Immaterial objects

An interface, a service, and a protocol have no physical body. Including them in a collection raises the question of what exactly is acquired — a right, a description, or a reproduction.

## 9. The university position

Working in an academic environment shifts the task from public to teaching. This is a different addressee and a different criterion of success from a museum.

## 10. Publication as result

A catalogue and research outlive a display. For a discipline contesting its academic status, publication is a weightier result than attendance.

## 11. An international perspective

Her projects consistently move beyond the Western canon. This requires research resource and connections, of which a design department usually has less than an art department.

## 12. What transfers

The strategy transfers: a stable programme, an acquisition budget, publication as a mandatory result. This is an administrative construction applicable in any institution.

## 13. What does not transfer

Position inside an institution does not transfer. The ability to run a programme depends on support from leadership and board, which does not follow from curatorial quality.

## 14. Critique

A justified objection: institutional curating of design depends on industry funding. Manufacturers of furniture and appliances support exhibitions about their own products.

## 15. A second objection

Showing contemporary practice brings a museum closer to the market: an exhibition raises the value of participants’ work. Distance here is structurally difficult.

## 16. The case for the defence

Transparency about funding sources and a published selection criterion mitigate the problem without removing it. It is the best available solution.

## 17. Historical position

Her work belongs to the period when design curating became an independent profession with a methodology of its own rather than a branch of art curating.

## 18. Link to the knowledge base

The material connects to the entries on the museum and design history, the exhibition, the vitrine as an architecture of attention, and corporate collections.

## 19. How to read this today

Look at a programme across five years rather than at an individual project. Continuity and the composition of acquisitions show an institution’s position more accurately than a headline exhibition.

## 20. A test question

Ask who funded an exhibition and whether that funder appears among the exhibitors. The answer determines the status of the judgement.
