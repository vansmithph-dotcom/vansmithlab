# Judith Clark

*Exhibition as a thinking device: fashion, space, scale and museum critique*

## Editorial thesis

Judith Clark treats the fashion exhibition not as a neutral container for beautiful clothes but as an independent medium of thought. In her projects display case, scale, copy, emptiness, text and visitor route become parts of an argument about memory, time and the ways museums produce fashion history.

## Reader question

What happens when a curator designs not only a list of objects but the very way a visitor is asked to doubt an exhibition?

## Short answer

Clark is a curator, researcher and teacher associated with London College of Fashion and the Centre for Fashion Curation. Since the late 1990s she has developed an experimental exhibition practice in which historical dress interacts with architecture, theatre, psychoanalysis, dictionaries and the conventions of the museum.

## 1. A small gallery as laboratory

In the late 1990s Clark opened the Judith Clark Costume Gallery in London, creating room to experiment with dress display outside a large museum structure. [^clm_vsl_cur_004_001]

## 2. Mechanism: exhibition as prototype

Small scale allows ideas to be tested quickly and the exhibition to operate as a prototype. The point is not an isolated gesture but a way of organising practice. The decision connects author, material, institution, technology and audience, so it cannot be reduced to a personal signature or a polished result.
