# Hans Ulrich Obrist

*Curating as the continuous production of connections, conversations, and archive rather than as the preparation of an individual exhibition.*

## Editorial thesis

Obrist matters for turning curatorial work from preparing displays into maintaining a network: his principal work is an accumulated corpus of conversations.

## Reader question

What does a curator produce besides exhibitions?

## Short answer

They produce connections and records: bringing together people who would not otherwise meet, and recording statements that would not otherwise survive.

## 1. Position

Obrist works as a curator and has long been associated with a major London gallery. His practice is unusual for its volume and continuity rather than for individual projects.

## 2. The interview as principal genre

Central to his work is a decades-long project of recording conversations with artists, architects, scientists, and designers. The corpus runs to thousands of hours.

## 3. Why this matters

A conversation records a position at the moment of its formation, before it becomes a canonical formulation. This is a source of a different kind from later recollection.

## 4. Accumulation as method

The corpus derives its value from volume: one interview is material, a thousand is a picture of an era. The method requires time and is therefore rarely reproduced.

## 5. The exhibition as network node

A display in his practice is a point of connection rather than a conclusion. A project continues through publications, conversations, and subsequent exhibitions.

## 6. Early projects outside institutions

He began with exhibitions in domestic spaces — a kitchen, a flat, a hotel room. This redefined where a display can occur at all.

## 7. The marathon as format

Multi-hour public conversation series turn discussion into an event with its own audience. The format borrows duration and publicity from the exhibition.

## 8. Interdisciplinarity as principle

He consistently brings artists together with scientists and architects. Productivity here is expected from the collision of vocabularies rather than from a shared subject.

## 9. The curator as public figure

His case documents the curator’s transformation into a recognisable name. This changed the profession: the curator became an author whose participation is itself an argument.

## 10. Speed and volume

His productivity is exceptional and is part of the method. Presence at many points simultaneously sustains a network that would otherwise dissolve.

## 11. The archive as work

The recorded conversations form an archive whose significance will grow. It is a deferred result, difficult to assess at the moment of production.

## 12. Attitude to design

Design and architecture enter his field on equal terms with art. This widening of participants is itself a curatorial decision.

## 13. What transfers

The format transfers: systematically recording conversations with practitioners as a way of preserving knowledge that does not reach publications. The method applies in any discipline.

## 14. What does not transfer

Access does not transfer. The ability to record the right person is secured by a position in the network, accumulated over decades.

## 15. Critique

A justified objection: quantity displaces selection. A continuously growing corpus ceases to be a curatorial statement and becomes an accumulation.

## 16. A second objection

The network reproduces itself: those already inside are invited. The widening of participants proceeds more slowly than the growth of volume.

## 17. A third objection

The conversation format does not accommodate objection. An interviewer maintaining relations with everyone rarely asks an uncomfortable question, and the corpus turns out uncritical.

## 18. Historical position

He is the limit case of curating as network activity. The model has spread widely, and its limitations are visible precisely at his scale.

## 19. Link to the knowledge base

The material connects to the entries on the exhibition, the museum and canon, corporate collections, and the archive as infrastructure.

## 20. How to read this today

Use the corpus as a source rather than as an assessment. A recorded statement is valuable in itself; the selection of speakers requires verification of its own.
