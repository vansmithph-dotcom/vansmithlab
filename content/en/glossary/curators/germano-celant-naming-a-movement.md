# Germano Celant

*The critic who gave a movement its name and thereby made it exist for history.*

## Editorial thesis

Celant matters as a case in which the act of naming proved equivalent to creation: a group of scattered practices became a movement at the moment a term appeared.

## Reader question

What does a critic do by introducing a new name for work that already exists?

## Short answer

They produce a boundary: determining what is included and what stays outside, and setting the language in which the work will subsequently be discussed.

## 1. Position

Celant worked as a critic and curator and is associated chiefly with Italian postwar art. His case is notable for combining criticism, curating, and institution building.

## 2. The name as act

In the late 1960s he introduced a term for a circle of Italian artists working with simple and unprocessed materials. The term took hold and determined how the work was received.

## 3. What a name does

It turns a coincidence into a programme. Artists working independently become participants in a shared movement, and subsequent history is written accordingly.

## 4. Material as content

The movement was defined by rejecting industrial and expensive materials in favour of earth, cloth, wood, and waste. The choice of material was a political statement.

## 5. A context of resistance

The position formed in opposition to industrial optimism and the art market system. Poor material was an argument against the commodity status of the work.

## 6. The paradox of its subsequent fate

Works conceived as anti-market became expensive museum objects. This is the usual fate of a radical gesture and is part of the material rather than a reproach to the artists.

## 7. The critic as participant

He did not describe the movement from outside but formed it: selecting participants, organising exhibitions, publishing texts. The role here is closer to producer than to analyst.

## 8. The problem with that role

A critic who created a movement cannot be its independent historian. Subsequent scholarship has to separate facts from his own construction.

## 9. Who was left outside

Every name excludes. Artists working similarly but not admitted to the circle fell outside the movement’s history, and restoring their presence is separate work.

## 10. Working with a corporate foundation

In a later period he directed the exhibition programme of a foundation created by a fashion house. This carried his method into an environment with different funding and different aims.

## 11. Reconstruction as genre

One of his known devices was the precise reproduction of historical exhibitions in a new space. This turns a display into an object of research.

## 12. What reconstruction proves

It shows that an exhibition is a work with authorship and composition rather than a neutral presentation. Arrangement carries meaning on equal terms with the exhibits.

## 13. The archive as foundation

His practice relied on documents, plans, and photographs of past displays. Without an archive reconstruction would be invention, and this is a condition of the method.

## 14. What transfers

The device of reconstruction as a research instrument transfers. It applies to the history of design and fashion, where an exhibition is likewise an authored work.

## 15. What does not transfer

The position of movement-creator does not transfer. It is possible in a small milieu at an early stage and is not reproduced in a developed institutional system.

## 16. Critique

A justified objection: concentrating the roles of critic, curator, and historian in one person deprives a movement’s history of independent verification. His version long remained the only one.

## 17. A second objection

Working with a corporate foundation raises the question of independent judgement. An institution funded by a fashion house determines significance and simultaneously profits from it.

## 18. Historical position

His case is useful for discussing how art history is produced: not by discovery but by naming, selection, and repetition.

## 19. Link to the knowledge base

The material connects to the entries on radical design, the exhibition as argument, the museum and canon, and corporate collections.

## 20. How to read this today

Ask who gave a phenomenon its name and what remained outside the term. The answer usually explains the structure of a history better than a list of participants.
