# Glenn Adamson

*Craft as a form of knowledge rather than a category of handmade objects.*

## Editorial thesis

Adamson matters for redefining craft from a category of things into a way of thinking: knowledge about material distributed in hands rather than in texts.

## Reader question

What does a society lose when it stops understanding what its things are made of?

## Short answer

It loses the ability to assess, repair, and contest: without an idea of material, judgement about an object reduces to price and appearance.

## 1. Position

Adamson works as a curator and historian specialising in craft and material culture. His contribution is chiefly theoretical: he changed the definition of his field’s subject.

## 2. Craft as process

He proposed treating craft not as a class of objects but as a mode of work present in any production. An industrial product also contains craft knowledge — in tooling and setup.

## 3. Rejecting the opposition

He regards the traditional opposition between craft and industry as false. Both categories describe the degree and distribution of skill, not different worlds.

## 4. Craft as supplement

He described craft as something always defined through something else: supplementary to art, to design, to industry. Hence its chronic undervaluation.

## 5. Material intelligence

A later concept of his describes the capacity to understand substance: where it comes from, how it was worked, how it will behave over time. He regards this knowledge as being lost and as socially significant.

## 6. A practical consequence

Losing that knowledge makes repair and informed purchase impossible. The argument here is applied rather than nostalgic: ignorance of material has an economic cost.

## 7. The exhibition as method

A substantial part of his work is curatorial projects in which a claim is tested through selection and arrangement. The exhibition here is a form of argument rather than an illustration of a text.

## 8. Joining writing and display

He works consistently in both formats and they refine one another. Text describes what an exhibition shows, and the reverse.

## 9. Working with a museum collection

Curatorial practice in applied art collections gives access to objects unavailable through publications. The argument here is built on inspection rather than on images.

## 10. Craft and the market

He analyses how craft origin is used to justify price. An appeal to handwork is a market instrument, and this should be stated directly.

## 11. Attitude to luxury rhetoric

He takes a critical view of brands with industrial volumes using a craft vocabulary. The frame of craft conceals the scale of production.

## 12. What transfers

The definition of craft as knowledge transfers. It allows skill to be discussed in any production, including digital, and removes a false opposition.

## 13. What does not transfer

Access to objects does not transfer. His method requires work with a collection, and without it reasoning about material remains bookish.

## 14. Critique

A justified objection: expanding craft to cover any skilled action deprives it of distinguishing power. If craft is everywhere, the term stops working.

## 15. A second objection

Discussion of material knowledge slips easily toward consumer advice. The boundary between criticism and recommendation here is thin.

## 16. Link to sustainability

Material knowledge is a precondition of repair and extended service life. His line converges with the regulatory agenda without being derived from it.

## 17. Historical position

He belongs to the generation that returned craft to academic discussion as a subject rather than a hobby. That is an institutional achievement as well as a theoretical one.

## 18. Link to the knowledge base

The material connects to the entries on craft and digital mastery, materials, sustainability, and the museum as producer of canon.

## 19. How to read this today

Test craft claims by asking about skill: what specific competence was required and where was it applied. The answer separates craft from rhetoric.

## 20. A test question

Ask not whether something is handmade but what exactly is handmade and in what quantity. The first question almost always receives a yes and is therefore useless.
