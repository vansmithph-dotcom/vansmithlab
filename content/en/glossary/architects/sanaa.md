# SANAA: Kazuyo Sejima and Ryue Nishizawa

*Transparency, thinness and the architecture of non-hierarchical space*

> SANAA creates an architecture that looks light but rests on complex control of structure, light, visibility and movement. Its space does not dictate a single primary route but offers a multitude of equally valid ways of being present.

## Editorial thesis

SANAA's significance rests not on 'white minimalism' as an external style. The practice investigates how thin structures, glass, reflection, free geometry and the absence of a single centre create open social relations.

## Reader question

How did two Japanese architects turn transparency, thinness and the free plan into one of the most influential languages of twenty-first-century public architecture?

The partners' personal practices are considered only in connection with the joint method.

Transparency and lightness are analysed as spatial and structural categories.

## Short answer

Kazuyo Sejima was born in 1956, studied at Japan Women's University and worked for Toyo Ito before opening her own office in 1987. Ryue Nishizawa was born in 1966 and began working with Sejima in the early 1990s. In 1995 they founded SANAA.

The 21st Century Museum of Contemporary Art in Kanazawa, the Glass Pavilion in Toledo, the New Museum, the Rolex Learning Center, Louvre-Lens, Grace Farms, the Bocconi campus and Sydney Modern demonstrate the development of their method. In 2010 the partners received the Pritzker Prize, and in 2025 SANAA was awarded the Royal Gold Medal for Architecture.

## 1. Kazuyo Sejima: education and Toyo Ito

Kazuyo Sejima was born in Ibaraki Prefecture in 1956 and received her architectural education at Japan Women's University.

After her studies she worked in the office of Toyo Ito, where she encountered the experimental Japanese architecture of the 1980s, oriented towards lightness and a changing urban image.

In 1987 Sejima founded her own office and began developing a language of thin surfaces and unconventional relationships between rooms.

## 2. Ryue Nishizawa and the partnership

Ryue Nishizawa was born in 1966 and educated at Yokohama National University.

He joined Sejima's studio, and their collaborative work gradually became a distinct authorial system.

Later Nishizawa opened his own office while retaining SANAA as the platform for large international projects.

## 3. The founding of SANAA

In 1995 Sejima and Nishizawa founded SANAA — Sejima and Nishizawa and Associates.

The joint practice made it possible to take part in international competitions and to carry out projects at a larger scale.

The name records the dual authorship and simultaneously designates a collective studio.

## 4. Early houses and the thin envelope

Early projects investigated small houses, workshops and residential buildings.

Walls could be very thin, rooms could be independent volumes, and the external contour could be almost neutral.

These experiments prepared the public projects in which lightness became a means of organising social relations.

## 5. 21st Century Museum of Contemporary Art

The museum in Kanazawa opened in 2004.

The circular single-storey building has no main façade. Galleries are placed within the transparent periphery as autonomous volumes.

The visitor can enter from different directions and choose a route, so the museum operates as an urban network.

## 6. The circle and the absence of a main façade

The circular form eliminates a privileged side of the building.

The transparent perimeter links the museum with the city, and public functions are placed between the galleries.

Non-hierarchical organisation offers freedom but can complicate orientation.

## 7. Glass Pavilion

The Glass Pavilion at the Toledo Museum of Art opened in 2006.

Curved glass walls form rooms, passages and courtyards without a traditional massive boundary.

Reflections and overlays make several spaces visible at once, rendering depth ambiguous.

## 8. New Museum

The New Museum opened on the Bowery in 2007.

The building consists of shifted boxes clad in an aluminium mesh.

The vertical volume responds to the dense Manhattan site while preserving flexible galleries and gaps of light.

## 9. Rolex Learning Center

The Rolex Learning Center on the EPFL campus opened in 2010.

A large continuous slab rises and falls, forming interior hills, courtyards and zones of varying openness.

The library, learning, dining and sociability are not separated by rigid floors; the space works as a landscape.

## 10. The continuous floor and the free route

The sloping floor removes the habitual division into rooms and levels.

The user sees distant parts of the building and chooses a trajectory, but physical movement demands a precise accessibility solution.

Freedom of route becomes real only when it is available to people with different mobility.

## 11. Louvre-Lens

Louvre-Lens opened in 2012 on the site of a former mine.

Low volumes of glass and reflective aluminium almost dissolve into the landscape.

The Galerie du Temps presents works from different eras in a single long space, loosening the traditional museum division.

## 12. Grace Farms

Grace Farms in Connecticut opened in 2015.

A series of pavilions is connected by a single sinuous River roof that follows the terrain.

The project unites cultural, communal, sporting and spiritual functions while keeping a significant portion of the site open.

## 13. Bocconi New Urban Campus

The new Bocconi University campus in Milan opened in 2020.

A system of rounded buildings and courtyards unites a residence hall, a sports centre and teaching spaces.

The campus is conceived as an urban fragment, not a closed institutional territory.

## 14. Sydney Modern Project

The new building of the Art Gallery of New South Wales opened in 2022.

Low pavilions descend the slope towards the harbour, linking up with terraces and the landscape.

A former oil storage tank has been converted into an underground exhibition space.

## 15. Transparency and the boundary

SANAA's transparency does not mean the complete absence of a boundary.

Glass reflects, separates climate and sound, and visibility depends on light.

The projects work as a system of partial connections and overlays, not of absolute openness.

## 16. Thinness and structural complexity

Visually thin columns, roofs and walls demand complex engineering coordination.

Lightness is the result of calculation, prototyping and precise manufacture.

The international realisation of SANAA's language depends on local engineers and building standards.

## 17. Non-hierarchical space

SANAA often avoids a central hall, a main axis and an obligatory route.

Functions are placed side by side, separated by transparent surfaces, distance or a change of floor level.

The system supports diverse use but can create difficulties of navigation and acoustics.

## 18. Collective authorship

Sejima and Nishizawa simultaneously lead SANAA and their own offices.

This makes it possible to separate small personal projects and large joint works.

Each building demands precise attribution of the team, engineers and local partners.

## 19. International recognition

In 2010 Kazuyo Sejima and Ryue Nishizawa received the Pritzker Architecture Prize.

Sejima became director of the Venice Architecture Biennale in the same year.

In 2025 SANAA received the Royal Gold Medal for Architecture.

## 20. Criticism and limitations

SANAA's architecture has been criticised for sterility, complexity of orientation and dependence on ideal execution.

Large expanses of glass can create climatic, acoustic and maintenance problems.

Superficial copying of white thin forms often loses the social and spatial sense of the original method.

## 21. Conclusion

SANAA has shown that lightness can be a way of reducing architectural hierarchy.

Their buildings offer a multitude of routes, views and forms of shared presence.

The practice's chief legacy is a space that strives not to prescribe behaviour but to create conditions for choice.
