# Herzog & de Meuron

*Material, envelope, transformation, and the architecture of mutable identity*

> "Herzog & de Meuron did not create a single external style. Their architecture changes from project to project because material, image, the existing building, the city, and the cultural program are assembled into a new system each time."

## Editorial thesis

The significance of Herzog & de Meuron rests on a radical investigation of surface, material, image, and the transformation of existing structures. The practice is not tied to a single form: it can be almost archaeological, like Tate Modern; crystalline, like Prada Aoyama; or infrastructural, like the Beijing stadium.

## Reader question

How did two architects from Basel turn material experiment and collaboration with artists into an international practice capable of working with museums, stadiums, housing, fashion brands, and urban districts?

To present Jacques Herzog and Pierre de Meuron as co-founders of a collective practice investigating materiality, image, and context. The text distinguishes personal biographies, the work of the office, and the contributions of partners, artists, engineers, and clients.

Primary subject: Jacques Herzog, Pierre de Meuron, and the practice Herzog & de Meuron from 1978 to 2026.

Projects are examined as the collective work of partners and teams, not as the result of two isolated authors.

The concept of the 'envelope' is analyzed alongside construction, climate, image, and urban role.

Collaboration with artists and fashion institutions is not described as secondary decoration.

The political and labor context of large international projects is considered separately from formal analysis.

The current composition of the practice and its projects carry a snapshot date and require updating before publication.

## Short answer

Jacques Herzog and Pierre de Meuron were born in Basel in 1950, studied at ETH Zürich, and in 1978 founded their joint practice. Early projects investigated ordinary industrial materials, concrete printing, translucent envelopes, and the connection of architecture to contemporary art.

The office's international influence was cemented by Dominus Winery, Tate Modern, Prada Aoyama, Allianz Arena, Beijing National Stadium, Elbphilharmonie, the Tate Modern extension, and M+. In 2001, Herzog and de Meuron received the Pritzker Prize. As of the verification date, both remain founding partners of Herzog & de Meuron.

## 1. Basel, ETH Zürich, and the founding of the practice

Jacques Herzog and Pierre de Meuron were born in Basel in 1950. They studied architecture together at ETH Zürich under Aldo Rossi and Dolf Schnebli.

In 1978, they founded their joint practice in Basel. The early decision to work as a permanent duo became the basis of an unusual model of authorship.

The Basel environment — pharmaceutical industry, museums, collectors, and a strong art scene — provided the context for linking architecture, production, and art.

## 2. Early minimalism and the industrial context

Herzog & de Meuron's early buildings often feature simple volumes and an almost anonymous geometry.

Their distinction is revealed in the material: the method of laying, the reflection, the printing, the perforation, and the way the surface changes over time.

The practice used industrial typologies not as a neutral functional shell but as a field of precise visual experiment.

## 3. Ricola and material as image

The series of projects for Ricola became an important laboratory for the office. Warehouses and production buildings received envelopes of wood, polycarbonate, concrete, and printed images.

In Ricola Europe in Mulhouse, translucent panels carry a repeating vegetal motif developed with photographs by Karl Blossfeldt as the image source.

The facade ceases to be merely a boundary: it becomes simultaneously material, scaled-up image, and light filter.

## 4. Dominus Winery

Dominus Winery in California was completed in 1998.

Gabion walls are filled with local stone of varying density. They shield against heat, allow air to pass, and change transparency depending on the light.

The project shows how a simple industrial element can become a climatic, constructive, and visual system.

## 5. Collaboration with artists

Herzog & de Meuron have consistently worked with Rémy Zaugg, Thomas Ruff, Ai Weiwei, and other artists.

Such collaboration changed not only decorative elements but also the way of seeing architecture, the city, and the viewer.

The dialogue with Rémy Zaugg was especially important, influencing the understanding of perception, text, space, and the institution.

## 6. Tate Modern

Tate Modern opened in 2000 in the former Bankside Power Station in London.

The architects preserved the scale of the Turbine Hall, the brick envelope, and the industrial memory, adding a light volume above the roof and new galleries.

The project proved that the transformation of existing infrastructure can be more powerful than full demolition and a new museum object.

## 7. Adaptive reuse and cultural regeneration

Tate Modern became a model of cultural regeneration in which the museum activates a former industrial site.

However, the success of a cultural institution can be accompanied by rising land values, touristification, and the displacement of prior functions.

Therefore, adaptive reuse should be evaluated not only as the conservation of material but also as a shift in the urban economy.

## 8. Prada Aoyama

Prada Aoyama in Tokyo was completed in 2003.

A rhomboid glass grid simultaneously forms the structure, the facade, and the display system. Convex, concave, and flat panels distort reflections and gazes.

The shop becomes an urban optical machine in which merchandise, visitor, and street continually observe one another.

## 9. Allianz Arena

Allianz Arena in Munich opened in 2005.

An envelope of pneumatic ETFE cushions can be illuminated, transforming the stadium into a changing urban sign.

The facade does not literally express the internal structure but creates a collective identity of the event through color and light.

## 10. Beijing National Stadium

The Beijing National Stadium, known as the Bird's Nest, was created by Herzog & de Meuron together with the China Architecture Design & Research Group and artistic consultant Ai Weiwei.

The interlaced steel structure unifies the load-bearing system, the facade, and the image.

The stadium became a symbol of the 2008 Olympic Games and one of the office's most recognizable structures.

## 11. Politics and labor of the mega-event

An Olympic stadium cannot be analyzed only as an engineering form. It is tied to state representation, enormous budgets, and the labor of construction workers.

Ai Weiwei later distanced himself from the official use of the project, criticizing the political context of the Olympics.

Architectural value and political instrumentalization can exist simultaneously and demand separate but linked analysis.

## 12. 1111 Lincoln Road

1111 Lincoln Road in Miami Beach was completed in 2010.

A multistory parking structure is designed as an open concrete framework with varying floor heights, public events, and commercial functions.

The project transforms a utilitarian typology into an urban stage but simultaneously reveals architecture's dependence on the automobile economy.

## 13. Parrish Art Museum

Parrish Art Museum in Water Mill opened in 2012.

A long low volume with a pitched roof interprets the agricultural structures of Long Island.

The repetitive construction creates a clear museum system and reduces the need for demonstrative sculptural form.

## 14. Tate Modern Switch House

The Tate Modern extension, later named the Blavatnik Building, opened in 2016.

A new pyramidal volume is clad with a perforated brick lattice and linked to the former oil tanks of the power station.

The project continues the logic of the existing material but forms an independent vertical structure.

## 15. Elbphilharmonie

The Elbphilharmonie in Hamburg opened in 2017.

A new glass volume of the concert hall is set on top of the brick warehouse Kaispeicher A. Between them lies a public Plaza.

The project became an important urban symbol but was accompanied by significant delays and cost increases, making it an example of simultaneous architectural success and managerial crisis.

## 16. M+ in Hong Kong

M+ opened in 2021 in the West Kowloon Cultural District.

A horizontal museum volume is intersected by a vertical tower, and a large media facade faces the harbor.

The building brings together exhibitions, storage, research, media, and urban communication but exists in the complex political context of Hong Kong.

## 17. Material, image, and envelope

Herzog & de Meuron treat the surface as an active architectural system.

The facade can be printed, illuminated, breathing, reflective, aging, deforming an image, or becoming part of the structure.

This is why their buildings are hard to unite under a single style: what is constant is not form but an investigative attitude toward the material image.

## 18. Urban projects and mixed-use

The practice works not only with individual objects but also with large districts, master plans, and mixed-use programs.

Such projects unite housing, culture, retail, offices, and public spaces.

The large scale amplifies the office's influence but also makes it a participant in land capitalization and shifts in the social structure of neighborhoods.

## 19. The collective authorship of the practice

Herzog & de Meuron has grown into an international practice with partners, associate partners, and large project teams.

Jacques Herzog and Pierre de Meuron remain founding partners, but specific projects are led by different partners.

Correct attribution must include the architectural team, local partners, engineers, artists, and the client.

## 20. The Pritzker Prize and teaching

In 2001, Jacques Herzog and Pierre de Meuron received the Pritzker Architecture Prize.

They taught at Harvard GSD and ETH Zürich and participated in the creation of ETH Studio Basel, which investigated the transformation of the contemporary territory.

Teaching and research maintained the link between design practice and urban analysis.

## 21. Critique and limitations

The office is criticized for participation in large luxury, sports, and state projects in which architecture becomes an instrument of branding and political representation.

Complex envelopes can demand expensive production, maintenance, and specialized materials.

Another contradiction is tied to the authorial brand: the diversity of collective work is often perceived through the names of the two founders.

## 22. Conclusion: architecture without a single style

Herzog & de Meuron have shown that a recognizable practice need not repeat a single form.

Their identity arises from the continual reinvention of relationships among material, image, construction, art, and the city.

The practice's principal legacy is the capacity to transform the surface and the existing building into carriers of a new cultural and spatial program.
