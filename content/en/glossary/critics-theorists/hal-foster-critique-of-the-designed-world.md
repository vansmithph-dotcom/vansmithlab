# Hal Foster

*A critique of the condition in which everything turns out to be designed, including the consumer.*

## Editorial thesis

Foster matters for describing the spread of design into every area of life as a problem rather than as a professional achievement.

## Reader question

What happens when not only an object is designed but also the person who uses it?

## Short answer

The vantage point from which criticism is possible disappears: a subject entirely styled by design has no position outside it.

## 1. Position

Foster works as an art historian and critic writing on modernism, postwar art, and architecture. Design enters his field as part of a broader discussion of visual culture.

## 2. Total design

His central observation on this subject is the spread of design intervention into everything, including the consumer’s body and biography. Design has stopped being a field and become a condition.

## 3. A historical parallel

He links the contemporary condition to the idea of the total work of art, in which an author designs an entire environment. The difference is that the designer is now the market.

## 4. The consumer as project

When a person styles themselves through the choice of objects, they become a product of the same process. The distinction between designer and designed disappears.

## 5. The loss of distance

The main consequence is the disappearance of an external position. Criticism is possible only from where not everything has been styled, and such places are diminishing.

## 6. Attitude to architecture

He criticises architecture that has become the production of a recognisable image. A building designed for the photograph serves media rather than use.

## 7. A dispute with postmodernism

He consistently analysed postmodern citation, showing where it constitutes criticism and where styling. That distinction remains a working instrument.

## 8. The return of the real

He described art’s attempt to break through to unstyled experience via trauma and the body. This is the reverse side of total design and a reaction to it.

## 9. Attitude to the profession

His criticism is addressed not to designers but to the conditions of their work. The accusation here is structural, which distinguishes him from a moralising line.

## 10. Method

He works through analysis of specific works and exhibitions rather than through general construction. The argument is built on examples and is therefore verifiable.

## 11. Usefulness for a designer

His texts give the profession an external optics it does not itself possess. A discipline discussing itself from inside systematically overestimates its own independence.

## 12. What transfers

The question of distance transfers: from where exactly is an assessment made, and is the assessor’s position part of the system described. This applies to any critical text.

## 13. What does not transfer

A ready conclusion does not transfer. His diagnosis describes a condition and offers no professional programme, and demanding one from him is misplaced.

## 14. Critique

A justified objection: describing total styling leaves no room for action. A position from which everything is already lost sits badly with practice.

## 15. A second objection

His optics work better on architecture and art than on product design, where production constraints act more strongly than cultural ones.

## 16. The case for the defence

A diagnosis need not contain a cure. The value of his texts is that they name a condition the profession is inclined not to notice.

## 17. Historical position

He represents a line in which design is treated as part of critical theory rather than as a separate applied discipline. That frame remains rare and therefore useful.

## 18. Link to the knowledge base

The material connects to the entries on postmodernism, citation and copying, the store as media, and architecture producing images.

## 19. How to read this today

Read him as a test of one’s own position. The most useful effect of his texts is discovering what had been taken for a neutral view.

## 20. A test question

Ask who commissioned an image and to whom it is addressed. That question reproduces his method at an applied scale.
