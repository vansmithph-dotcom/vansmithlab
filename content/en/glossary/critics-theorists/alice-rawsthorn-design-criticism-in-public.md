# Alice Rawsthorn

*Design criticism addressed to a wide audience, insisting that design is change rather than styling.*

## Editorial thesis

Rawsthorn matters for taking design criticism out of a professional circle into public and insisting on a definition of design through the intention to change rather than through appearance.

## Reader question

Why does a society need design criticism if objects sell anyway?

## Short answer

Because design distributes access, safety, and attention. Decisions taken by designers act on everyone and are therefore subject to public discussion.

## 1. Position

Rawsthorn works as a design critic writing for a wide audience. Her addressee is not the professional community, and this determines her mode of exposition.

## 2. Defining the discipline

She defines design through the intention to produce change rather than through a category of object. Such a definition includes what usually falls outside: a protocol, a service, a sign.

## 3. A consequence of the definition

If design is change, it is not confined to a profession. Much of the most consistent design has been made by people who did not call themselves designers.

## 4. Expanding the canon

She consistently brings into discussion figures and objects outside the traditional list: hazard signs, pirate flags, engineering solutions. This is work on widening the discipline’s subject.

## 5. Criticism as a public genre

Design criticism exists in the shadow of architectural and art criticism. Part of her work consists in establishing the genre as such.

## 6. The regular column as form

Sustained work in a column for an international publication gave the discipline a permanent place in public discussion. Regularity here matters more than any individual text.

## 7. Working with institutions

She participates in museum and educational boards. The critic here does not only describe but participates in decisions, which creates a familiar tension.

## 8. A project on crises

A collaborative project on design’s role in responding to public crises was conducted as conversations with practitioners. The interview format was chosen as a way to gather distributed knowledge.

## 9. The interview as method

Conversation allows a practitioner’s position to be recorded before it becomes canon. This differs from historical analysis and serves a different purpose.

## 10. Attitude to the market

She consistently separates the assessment of design from commercial success. A well-selling object may be a poor solution, and this claim has to be repeated constantly.

## 11. Technology in her frame

New technologies are treated as a change of conditions rather than as a value in themselves. The question posed is whose access widened and whose narrowed.

## 12. What transfers

The definition through intention transfers. It allows things with no designer-author to be discussed as design and widens the subject of conversation.

## 13. What does not transfer

The position of public critic does not transfer. It requires a platform with an audience, and in design very few such platforms exist.

## 14. Critique

A justified objection: a broad definition of design dilutes the discipline. If design is any intentional change, the concept loses its power to distinguish.

## 15. A second objection

Participation in institutional boards complicates critical distance. A critic sitting on juries and boards assesses an environment of which they are part.

## 16. A response to the objection

In design the narrowness of the professional circle makes full independence practically unattainable. Acknowledging the conflict is more honest than denying it.

## 17. Historical position

She belongs to the generation that established design criticism as a public genre with an audience of its own. Before this the conversation ran mostly inside the profession.

## 18. Link to the knowledge base

The material connects to the entries on sustainability, the museum and canon, design systems, and designer responsibility.

## 19. How to read this today

Test her definition on specific cases. It is productive where the object is obvious and requires caution where intention is attributed retrospectively.

## 20. A test question

Ask what exactly changed and for whom. A definition through intention requires verification of outcome, or it remains rhetoric.
