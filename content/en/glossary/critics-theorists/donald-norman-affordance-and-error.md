# Donald Norman

*User error as a defect of design rather than a property of the user.*

## Editorial thesis

Norman matters for moving responsibility for error from the person to the object, and for giving the profession a vocabulary in which to discuss it.

## Reader question

Who is at fault when a person cannot open a door?

## Short answer

The door: if its construction does not communicate how it is used, the error is programmed into it and will recur for most users.

## 1. Position

Norman works as a cognitive scientist and design theorist. His contribution consists in carrying concepts from the psychology of perception into an applied discipline.

## 2. Error as defect

The central claim: if users systematically make the same mistake, the cause lies in the object. This moves blame from person to construction and makes error measurable.

## 3. Affordance

The key concept describes properties of an object that suggest a possible way of handling it. A flat plate implies pushing, a handle pulling; a conflict between these signals produces error.

## 4. A refinement of the concept

He later clarified that perceived rather than physical affordance matters more for design. What counts is not what an object permits but what a user believes possible.

## 5. The signifier

To remove confusion he introduced a separate concept for explicit cues: labels, arrows, markers. An affordance exists objectively; a signifier communicates it.

## 6. Mapping

The relation between the arrangement of controls and of controlled objects determines the number of errors. Hob burners and their knobs are the canonical example of poor mapping.

## 7. Feedback

A system must report that an action has registered. An absent response produces repeated pressing and a subsequent error — a described and reproducible mechanism.

## 8. Constraint

Construction can physically preclude an incorrect action: a connector that fits one way only removes the question of instruction. This is the most reliable form of design protection.

## 9. The conceptual model

A user builds an idea of how a device works and acts according to it. The designer’s task is to make that idea coincide with the actual mechanism.

## 10. The door as example

The example of a door with misleading cues became proverbial and entered professional language. A good example distributes a theory further than an exposition.

## 11. Human-centred design

He formalised an approach in which requirements are derived from observing users. The method became an industry standard in digital development.

## 12. Critique of his own approach

He himself noted that human-centredness applied literally leads to improving the existing rather than reconsidering the task. That is an honest limitation of the method.

## 13. The emotional component

In later work he added that objects are judged by more than efficiency: attractiveness affects tolerance of difficulty. This refines rather than cancels the earlier claim.

## 14. What transfers

The vocabulary transfers. Affordance, signifier, mapping, feedback, and constraint are five concepts allowing construction to be discussed without appeal to taste.

## 15. What does not transfer

The priority of clarity over everything else does not transfer. There are categories in which difficulty is the content: games, ritual, learning.

## 16. Critique

A justified objection: the approach optimises the existing and generates the new poorly. Observing users gives no answer where a practice does not yet exist.

## 17. A second objection

The concept of affordance is borrowed from ecological psychology and lost part of its original meaning in applied use. Terminological imprecision here is widespread and acknowledged.

## 18. Historical position

His vocabulary underlies contemporary interface design practice. Most professional disputes in that field are conducted in his terms.

## 19. Link to the knowledge base

The material connects to the entries on interfaces, design systems, screen graphics, and Ulm as a school of method.

## 20. How to read this today

Apply his concepts as diagnosis rather than prescription. They explain well why an object fails and less well what it should be.
